# Keita (1979), annexe : proverbes, contes, devinettes — working notes

> **Local working file, not distributed** (git-ignored). Companion to `Sekou Sondo Keita - kissie fondamental (annexe proverbes contes devinettes).pdf`, the annex of Keita's 1979 thesis (typescript pp. 166–184; listed in the thesis contents as "V- ANNEXE : Contes - Proverbes - Devinettes", missing from the main scan).
>
> - **What is here:** for each of the 12 tales, its title, typescript page and a short outline in our own words; then **every proverb** (*talelan*, each with its answer line *kölöö*) and **every riddle** (*budon pialan*, with the answer after *Mulio*) exactly as typed, with the legible handwritten French glosses. The tale prose itself is outlined, not copied.
> - **Spelling:** Keita's 1979 orthography (*ty, nh, ny, è, ö*), reproduced as typed, in NFC; typed underlining is not kept. Nothing is respelled here.
> - **Marks:** `[?]` illegible, `[…]` hidden by the ink stain or the cover stamp, `> ✎` handwritten note (French, by a later reader), `~~typed~~ <ins>handwritten</ins>` handwritten correction.
> - **Pages:** PDF p. 1 is a cover sheet with a stamp "PROVERBES – CONTES – DEVINETTES – ANNEXE" printed over a faint copy of p. 2. Typescript page numbers are visible on PDF pp. 4 (170), 8 (174), 9 (175), 10 (176), 14 (180) and 15 (181); PDF p. N = typescript p. N + 166.
> - **Outlines:** made by reading the 1979 Kisiei; they are approximate and give only the general line of each story.

## PDF p. 2 (typescript p. 168)

### POAA LEBOOLOA NDA NHION

*Outline:* Lankpaa, a man of rank (*lebolo*), and his children (*nhiön*); the sons are tested through a series of events in the village and the bush. A dilemma tale: it ends by asking the listeners « Poaa nhiönda a fallo, o nèè hiow yè lebolo ? » — which of the man's sons best deserves to be chief?

### TALELAN

- "Te yaw tembu le vii pim te".
  Kölöö : Vana nö se le a tiindan tyaanda.
  > ✎ l'enfant de chasseur n'a pas l'envie de manger la viande parce qu'il [?]
- "Tyua dosunöö nö ma hilu le, sölaa o tyo ni".
  > ✎ (above *hilu*) envie de viande
  Kölöö : Mèè vana dèli tyuamböö yè o nö kaa ni.
  > ✎ comme habitude de l'enfant, il a ce comportement

### BUDON PIALAN

- Piöö sia o sia le, kpèènèi kon nö mènndan löölöö. ( Mulio : Lombulten )
  > ✎ Halaa — la pluie pleut ou pleut pas, ce marmite a toujours l'eau — dumbullen = citron
- Tyièi kala ni pömbö kandun yindöö o lebol. ( Mulio : Hollo )
  > ✎ la maison de la petite mère (tante) commence par la tête — champignon

## PDF p. 3 (typescript p. 169)

### SOFELE NDA MASANO NDOO

*Outline:* a rich man (*Langbanöö*) and the boy Sofèlè, who serves him and is treated harshly; the boy's suffering is seen by God (« Hala a tye ! Hala a tye ! »), and the story turns the master's fortune. Ends with a moral on how one treats those in one's care.

### TALELAN

- "Vana velu pèndua pè, a hini nika matyali".
  Kölöö : Vana nö vènhaa nhiön pè, fö ma biilu nda, nda nhiön gbow nika.
- "Vana lem pè pangaa le muiyo, numpila kani : baw yinden le bi pè, muiyo köènnun, numpila koma tyal o bè."
  Kölöö : Vana kè mömöö yaw le miallo pè, te miallo köènnun yè.

### BUNDON PIALAN

(typed thus, "BUNDON")

- Könndan tan tiön, kumbinndo löölöö diompilè. ( Mulio : Hölta koongolan )

## PDF p. 4 (typescript p. 170)

*Outline:* in a time of hunger the animals (*visia*) go to the bush; Koli and San (the hare) compete for food, and San's trickery outwits the others. Ends with a moral on cunning and sharing.

## PDF p. 8 (typescript p. 174)

### TAMBAN KOLLO, YALAA A TYUILIN

*Outline:* in a famine, the animals Yalaa, Tambili and Tyuilin, with Koli, look for food; Tyuilin hides a store of food (*bandila*) and lies about it until found out. Ends with a moral on lying and sharing in hard times.

### TALELAN

- " Visia mèè n va yè lende n yinga ni "
  Kölöö : Vana löön takpa le nyè hiow mumndo le.
- " Taala bèndu tyo mömö bèndu le"
  Kölöö : Yoomuna tyo le va o böö le.

### BUDON PIALAN

- Yoolen le yii vannda faw . ( Mulio : Löön )
- Töönndo tyo o yönndo tyoo kè mi n pisul lèn a lomnde ndölen. ( Mulio : Paalen a suenna ndöön )

## PDF p. 9 (typescript p. 175)

### KOOMAA, PEWVO A FAL FODEENOO

*Outline:* a man of the Fodé family (*Fodenöö*) goes hunting with his dog Pèw; a spirit or animal, Köömaa, is caught and pleads for its life, and Tivoli also takes part. Explains a food taboo of the Fodé people (continued on p. 10).

## PDF p. 11 (typescript p. 177)

### TAMBILI NDA VISIA YONDOA

*Outline:* Tambili and the wild animals (*visia yondoa*): a contest of tricks in a time of scarcity; Tambili sings a song to lure the animals:

> Suluku bombole pömbö pololo / A tui isöö le / Tyangulu kpelenkpelen / Tisèè tisè tisè tisè (sung twice)

### SAANKANI (begins)

*Outline:* Saankani is a very rich man (*vanabolofaa bènduo*) with many wives and goods, but he does not know how to treat others; at a crisis no one stands by him and he loses everything, while his helper Bilama is the one who stays. Ends with a moral on wealth and friendship.
