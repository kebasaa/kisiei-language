    Alphabet¶

    The letters¶

    The Kisiei alphabet has 26 letters. The letters specific to Kisiei are ɛ, ɔ, ŋ and ɲ; the letters j, q, x and z are not used (they do not appear even once in the Bible (BKG)).

    Lower case Capital Proposed name Example Meaning
    a A a kolaa wrapper (cloth)
    b B be bɛndoo big
    c C ce cɛiyo house
    d D de diolan name
    e E e pelei foot
    ɛ Ɛ ɛ lɛŋnden country, land
    f F fe fuɲaa father
    g G ge tenga shine, be bright
    h H he hala God
    i I i pilɛ one
    k K ke kalaa mother
    l L le lɛŋ (on the) ground, soil
    m M me mɛnndan water
    n N ne naŋ good
    ŋ Ŋ ŋe ŋuɛɛnu five
    ɲ Ɲ ɲe ɲumndan night, darkness
    o O o yɔmndo tree
    ɔ Ɔ ɔ pɔmbɔɔ small, younger
    p P pe pee river
    r R re Abraham (proper name)
    s S se sɛbɛ book, letter
    t T te tiɔn two
    u U u cuaa child
    v V ve vana person
    w W we taw much, many
    y Y ye yáá three

    The letter names follow the usage described in 1979 (Keita 1979, p. 14): each consonant is pronounced followed by a short e (be, de, fe…) and not the French way (bé, dé, èf…).

    Double letters¶

    Several sounds are written with two letters. These are not sequences of two sounds, but a single consonant:

    Group Sound Example Meaning
    kp [k͡p] labial-velar stop kpemaa covenant
    gb [ɡ͡b] labial-velar stop langbanɔɔ adult man, elder
    mb [ᵐb] prenasalised pɔmbɔɔ small
    nd [ⁿd] prenasalised ndu him, her
    ng [ᵑɡ] prenasalised tenga shine
    ll [lː] long l hangullo heat

    The prenasalised consonants and kp are also analysed as single consonants in Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 24, 30-31). The ll, on the other hand, is described there as two ls belonging to two syllables (Childs 1995, p. 151) (see Consonants).

    Long vowels (aa, ee, ɛɛ, ii, oo, ɔɔ, uu) are described on the Vowels page.

    Letters with a restricted position¶

    Some letters appear only in certain positions in the word:

    • g appears only in ng and gb; no Kisiei word begins with g. In Southern Kissi, too, g does not exist on its own: an initial g in a loanword becomes k or ng there (Childs 1995, p. 25, 30).
    • w is written only after a vowel, at the end of a syllable: taw, kpow, nawvo. No word begins with w.
    • r appears only in proper names and loanwords: Abraham, rɛsɛn “grape”. Some Northern varieties pronounce r in ordinary Kisiei words, but the Bible writes l in them (see Regional varieties).
    • ŋ is found mainly at the end of a stem (naŋ, lɛŋ) and before n (lɛŋnden); at the beginning of a word it appears only in a few words, including the numbers ŋuɛɛnu “five” and ŋɔmpum “six”.

    Alphabetical order¶

    For sorting words (glossaries, indexes), the following order is proposed, in which each letter specific to Kisiei follows the closest Latin letter:

    a b c d e ɛ f g h i k l m n ŋ ɲ o ɔ p r s t u v w y

    The groups kp, gb, mb, nd, ng are sorted under their first letter (kpemaa under k). Tone accents are ignored in sorting.

    Note

    This order is a proposal of this guide; neither the 1979 manual nor the Bible gives one. See the open questions.
