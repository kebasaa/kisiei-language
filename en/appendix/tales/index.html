    Tales, proverbs and riddles¶

    The annex of Sekou Sondo Keita's thesis (Keita 1979) (“V- ANNEXE : Contes - Proverbes - Devinettes”, typed pages 166–184) brings together twelve tales in Kisiei. Each tale is followed by proverbs (talelan), each with its answer line (kölöö), and by riddles (budon pialan), whose answer follows the word Mulio.

    For this guide the proverbs and riddles are respelled in the Bible's orthography (see From the old to the new spelling); the form typed in 1979 is given in italics under each text. Words not found in the Bible are marked “respelled”, doubtful readings “to check” (see How to read this guide); “[…]” marks a passage hidden by an ink stain.

    The translations are the guide's own, unless marked “handwritten note”: the scanned copy carries glosses handwritten in French, some of them illegible ([?]); they are translated here from the French. Many of these texts are figurative and elliptic; the guide's translations are often approximate, and are marked (?) or “meaning uncertain” where they are.

    Sources: the scanned annex (PDF) and the archive's working notes, which give every proverb and riddle as typed and an outline of each tale.

    Proverbs (talelan)¶

    1. The sons, the chieftaincy and the children (?)¶

    Poaa lebooloa nda ŋiɔn (tale, p. 168)

    1. Te yaw tembu le, vii pim te. — kölöö: Vana nɔ se le a tiindan caanda.
    1979: “Te yaw tembu le vii pim te”. — Kölöö : Vana nö se le a tiindan tyaanda.
    Meaning uncertain (word for word: “if yaw does not wear out, vii is not satisfied”). — Kölöö: “One who has no means puts his hope in others.” (Keita 1979, p. 168)
    2. Cua doosunɔɔ nɔ ma hilu le, sɔlaa o co ni. — kölöö: Mɛɛ vana dɛli cuaambɔɔ yɛ, o nɔ kaa ni.
    1979: “Tyua dosunöö nö ma hilu le, sölaa o tyo ni”. — Kölöö : Mèè vana dèli tyuamböö yè o nö kaa ni.
    “The hunter's child does not crave meat: he always gets some.” — Kölöö: “As one trains a child, so he behaves.” (Keita 1979, p. 168)
    Handwritten notes: “the hunter's child has no craving to eat meat because he [?]”; above hilu: “craving for meat”; under the answer: “as the child is used to, so he behaves”.

    2. Sofɛlɛ and his master¶

    Sofɛlɛ nda masanɔ ndɔɔ (tale, p. 169)

    3. Vana velu pɛndua pɛ, a hini nika macali. — kölöö: Vana nɔ vɛŋaa ŋiɔn pɛ, fɔ ma biilu nda, nda ŋiɔn kpow nika.
    1979: “Vana velu pèndua pè, a hini nika matyali”. — Kölöö : Vana nö vènhaa nhiön pè, fö ma biilu nda, nda nhiön gbow nika.
    “He who has fathered twins lies thus, on his back (?).” — Kölöö: “One who has … children must honour them, them and all the children (?).” Meaning uncertain. (Keita 1979, p. 169)
    4. Vana lem pɛ pangaa le muiyo, numpila kani: baw yinnden le bi pɛ, muiyo kɔɛnnun, numpila koma cal o bɛ. — kölöö: Vana kɛ mɔmɔɔ yaw le miallo pɛ, te miallo kɔɛnnun yɛ.
    1979: “Vana lem pè pangaa le muiyo, numpila kani : baw yinden le bi pè, muiyo köènnun, numpila koma tyal o bè.” — Kölöö : Vana kè mömöö yaw le miallo pè, te miallo köènnun yè.
    Meaning uncertain: it speaks of a field shelter, mosquitoes (muiyo) and another insect (numpila); when a fire is lit the mosquitoes flee, but the numpila stays (?). — Kölöö: “If one does not cook a meal for the stranger, the stranger leaves (?).” (Keita 1979, p. 169)

    3. Vɛɛn and jealousy (?)¶

    Vɛɛn paŋalaa (tale, pp. 170–171)

    5. Nɛɛ vana kɛsi cuɛiyan nda, a vuu pow lon te. — kölöö: Nɛɛ vana kɛsi ɲɛ kɛndɔɔ va, a cɛl mi dɛnɛ vɔɔnda kɔɛ lon te.
    1979: “Nèè vana kèsi tyuèiyan nda, a vuu pow lon te”. — Kölöö : Nèè vana kèsi nyèkèndöö va, a tyèl mi dènèvöönda köè lon te.
    “Where oil has been set down, one does not throw stones.” — Kölöö: “Where good things have been put, one does not let bad things go.” (Keita 1979, p. 170)
    6. Siaŋaa válán co naŋɔɔ le. — kölöö: Naŋɔɔ lahɔl co kaa kɛndɛ ko le.
    1979: “Sianhaa valan tyo nanhöö le”. — Kölöö : Nanhöö lahöl tyo Kaa kèndè ko le
    “Bright red is not (necessarily) beautiful.” — Kölöö: meaning uncertain (Kaa is underlined in the typescript). (Keita 1979, p. 170)

    4. Leopard and Hare¶

    Tamban kollo nda Saŋ (tale, pp. 171–172)

    7. Sɔɔ mɛɛ o nɔla tɛɛndu yɛ, lende o dɔw ni. — kölöö: Mɛɛ n co yɛ, lende n tosa dɛntan ni.
    1979: “Söö mèè o nöla tèèndu yè, lende o döw ni”. — Kölöö : Mèè n tyo yè, lende ntosa dèntan ni.
    “As the hen can scratch (?), so she feeds (?).” — Kölöö: “As you are, so you act.” (Keita 1979, p. 172)
    8. Nile piɔnde tui le. — kölöö: Vana va pɛ le vuo, a dimul nɔm bɛɛ, a kɔa lon te, fɔ ma kɔɛ.
    1979: “Nile piönde tui le”. — Kölöö : Vana va pè le vuo, a dimul num bèè, a köa lon te, fö ma köè.
    “The … ear does not hear (?).” — Kölöö: “As long as one is not dead, even if you are told "do not go there", you must go (?).” Meaning uncertain. (Keita 1979, p. 172)

    5. Piɔmndo (a spirit) and the old man¶

    Piɔmndo nda fallo sindɛ (tale, pp. 172–173)

    9. Siɛmbulan bii mandenɔɔ le, kɛ ɲɛɛsuu tendei. — kölöö: Kaasonɔɔ pɔw coola ndɔlan te, mbo sɔlɔlan vɛlɛ le.
    1979: “Sèmbulan bii mandenöö le, kè nyèèsuu tendei”. — Kölöö : Kaasunöö pöw tyoola ndölan te, mbo sölölan vèlè le.
    “Shame does not seize the diviner, but … the forehead (?).” — Kölöö: “The young man who goes astray in his lies (?) no longer confesses them.” Meaning uncertain. (Keita 1979, p. 173)
    Handwritten note above mandenöö: “mandeŋ [?] — he keeps [?]”.
    10. Sɛngia sanaa co pee kɔngɔ le. — kölöö: Sɔɛi co deŋ ndo nɔ kɔɔsɔ le.
    1979: “Sèngia sanaa tyo pee köngö le ”. — Kölöö : Söèi tyo den ndo nö köösö le.
    Meaning uncertain (sanaa “straight”, pee kɔngɔ “on the river bank”). — Kölöö: meaning uncertain (sɔɛi “matter”, kɔɔsɔ “judgement”). (Keita 1979, p. 173)

    6. Leopard, Lion and Cuilin¶

    Tamban kollo, yàlàá a Cuilin (tale, p. 174)

    11. Visia mɛɛ n va yɛ, lende n yinga ni. — kölöö: Vana lɔɔn takpa le ɲɛ hiow mumndo le.
    1979: “ Visia mèè n va yè lende n yinga ni ”. — Kölöö : Vana löön takpa le nyè hiow mumndo le.
    “As you were (?), so you wake up (?).” — Kölöö: meaning uncertain (takpa “chest”, ɲɛ hiow “nothing surpasses”). (Keita 1979, p. 174)
    12. Taala bɛndu co mɔmɔ bɛndu le. — kölöö: Yoomuma co le va o bɔɔ le.
    1979: “ Taala bèndu tyo mömö bèndu le”. — Kölöö : Yoomuna tyo le va o böö le.
    “A big calabash is not a big meal.” — Kölöö: “Strength does not lie in size.” (Keita 1979, p. 174)
    Yoomuna is read as yoomuma “strength”.

    7. Kɔɔmaa, Pɛwvo and old Fodeenɔɔ¶

    Kɔɔmaa, Pɛwvo a fal Fodeenɔɔ (tale, pp. 175–176)

    13. Suvaa hiavannda […] ni. — kölöö: Vanaa sulannd…
    1979: “ Suwvaa hiavannda […] ni ”. — Kölöö : Vanaa sulannd[…].
    “Fish are passers-by …” (end hidden by the ink stain). — Kölöö: end hidden. (Keita 1979, p. 176)
    Handwritten note: “the fish [?] have passed — refused to be caught — the tyua”.
    14. Fodee di ncɔn te[…] […]aa ndɔa dia. — kölöö: Yeemia hɛli lepola le.
    1979: “ Fodee di ntyön te[…] […]aa ndöa dia ”. — Kölöö : Yeemia hèli lepola le.
    “The Fodé do not eat …” (partly hidden by the stain). — Kölöö: “A food taboo does not choose the family (?).” (Keita 1979, p. 176)
    The handwritten note is almost illegible under the stain.

    8. Tambili and the animals of the bush¶

    Tambili nda visia yondoa (tale, pp. 177–178)

    15. Bɛnda dii kana ni. — kölöö: Fàngà kunda bumba ɲɛ le.
    1979: “ Bènda dii kana ni ”. — Kölöö : Fanga kunda bumba nyè le.
    Meaning uncertain (bɛnda “must”, dii “kill”). — Kölöö: “The strength of the group fails at nothing (?).” (Keita 1979, p. 178)
    Typed koma, crossed out and corrected by hand to “[?] kana”.
    16. Vana nia kol mɔɛiyan nɔɛ pilɛ vo, o bɛnda mi la kol vɛlɛ nɔnɔan nia ndu. — kölöö: Vana va nɔm ikɛi o dɛnɛ vɔɔndo nin ndo, o bɛnda mi vana kon va nɔm ikɛi lɔɔ […] dɛnɛ kɛndɔɔ nin ndo.
    1979: “Vana nia kol möèiyan nöè pilè vo, o bènda mi la kol vèlè nönöan nia ndu”. — Kölöö : Vana va num ikèi o dènèvöndo nin ndo, o bènda mi vana kon va num ikèi löö […] dènèkèndö nin ndo.
    “The one you drank wine (?) with …, you must also drink milk with him.” — Kölöö: “The one who stood by you in bad times must also stand by you in good times.” (Keita 1979, p. 178)

    9. Saankani (a man's name)¶

    Saankani (tale, pp. 178–179)

    17. Ɲaayoo n kɔ o poli, hɔlla kan n cen ni. — kölöö: Vanndo sɔɛi dinullo, hɔlla kan n cen ni.
    1979: “Nyaayoo n kö o poli, hölla kan n tyen ni ”. — Kölöö : Vanndo söèi dinullo, hölla kan n tyen ni.
    Meaning uncertain (ɲaayoo “cat”, hɔlla “face”; both lines end with the same phrase). (Keita 1979, p. 179)
    18. Vana sin o pusuo coo le ma congu lɛŋnden te. — kölöö: Vana congu vanndo vana ndɔɔ hɔl te.
    1979: “ Vana sin o pusuo tyoo le ma tyongu lènnden te ”. — Kölöö : Vana tyongu vanndo vana ndöö höl te
    Meaning uncertain (congu lɛŋnden “speak ill of the country”?). — Kölöö: “One does not speak ill of someone in front of his own people.” (Keita 1979, p. 179)

    10. Hare and Hyena¶

    Saŋ nda Suluku (tale, pp. 180–181)

    19. A komba nɔm pɛ maa banda, del lɛ maa yɔm pɔmbɔɔ le. — kölöö: Vanndo, a ce nɔm pɛ maa vana bɛnduo, tosa lɛ dɛntan maa cuaambɔ le.
    1979: “ A komba num pè maa banda, del lè maa yön pömböö le ”. — Kölöö : Vanndo, a tye num pè maa vana bènduo, tosa lè dèntan maa tyuaambö le.
    “If you are felled like a great tree (?), do not fall like a small tree any more.” — Kölöö: “You, if people look on you as an elder, do not act like a child any more.” (Keita 1979, p. 181)
    Handwritten note: “you are praised, but you can do nothing — if you are cut like a [?], [?] small tree”.
    20. Kilta bɛnda a ɲasɔn te. — kölöö: Vana nɔla sɔɔn mundu mun nɛŋi o vati piloo bɛngu le.
    1979: “ Kilta bènda a nyasön te ”. — Kölöö : Vana nöla söön mundu mun nènhi o vati piloo bèngu le.
    Meaning uncertain (kilta “running”). — Kölöö: meaning uncertain. (Keita 1979, p. 181)

    11. Tambili and Cuil¶

    Tambili nda Cuil (tale, pp. 181–182)

    21. Cuilin co o le yoo le vo, bil oo bil ko o da. — kölöö: Vana nɔ yungu le, nɛnɛka o kɔa.
    1979: “ Tyuilin tyo o le yoo le vo, bil oo bil ko o da ”. — Kölöö : Vana nö yungu le, nènèka o köa.
    Meaning uncertain (bil oo bil “every grass”). — Kölöö: “One who has no family goes everywhere.” (Keita 1979, p. 182)
    Typed die, corrected by hand to da.
    22. Masaa bii sɔɛi vanakpafo le. — kölöö: Vana kunda bii sɔɛi vana pɔmbɔ le.
    1979: “ Masaa bii söèi vanagbafo le ”. — Kölöö : Vanakunda bii söèi vana pömbö le .
    “The chief does not heed the word of a … (?).” — Kölöö: “The great one does not heed the word of the small one.” (Keita 1979, p. 182)
    23. Kambalaa ben te, kɛ o co coo. — kölöö: Vana kala a cɛi nɔm te, kɛ a lɔl o luŋ te.
    1979: “ Kambalaa ben te, kè o tyo tyoo ”. — Kölöö : Vana kala a tyièi num te, kè a löl o lun te .
    Meaning uncertain (kambalaa “grasshopper”). — Kölöö: “Even without a house, one does not sleep in the public square.” (Keita 1979, p. 182)

    12. Laŋunɔɔ the hunter and the elephant¶

    Laŋunɔɔ doosunɔɔ nda kamaa (tale, pp. 183–184)

    24. Sondɛn balafa le nɛi le, yiminndo kuani. — kölöö: Vana cen le lɔɔ […] le, o nɛi bɛlɛ.
    1979: “ Sondèn barafa le nèi le, yiminndo kuani ”. — Kölöö : Vana tyen le löö […] le, o nèi bèlè .
    Meaning uncertain. — Kölöö, after the handwritten note: “Friends do not cry under blows, even if it hurts.” (Keita 1979, p. 184)
    Hand corrections: Sionnde → Sondèn, sama → barafa, and in the answer an illegible word replaces tiama. Notes: “the balondo [?] does [?] not hurt, you move the [?]”; “friends do not cry at blows even if it hurts”.
    25. Tun mial nɔ le lon te. — kölöö: Miallo hiw pilacieo le.
    1979: “ Tun mial nö le lon te ”. — Kölöö : Miallo hiw pilatyieo le
    “The stranger dog has no tail (?).” — Kölöö: “The stranger does not come before the native.” (Keita 1979, p. 184)
    Handwritten note: “the stranger dog has no tail”.
    26. Hɔlɔɔ dimi pɛ a fɔ ni i bandu maa pɛwvo, fɔ mbo kiolu tayɛn o kon. — kölöö: Vana bala fondoo dimi pɛ, a fɔ mi i tosa dɛntan maa vanabolofaa, a tambu.
    1979: “ Hölöö dimi pè a fö ni i bandu maa pèwvo, fö mbo kiolu tayèn o kon ”. — Kölöö : Vanabalafondoo dimi pè, a fö mi i tosa dèntan maa vanabolofaa, a tambu .
    “If the hɔlɔɔ says it must be as long as the python, it will end up cut … (?).” — Kölöö: “If the poor man says he must do as the rich man does, he is ruined.” (Keita 1979, p. 184)
    Note in the margin: “you cannot because friendship is hidden, one does not want to uncover it [?]”.

    Riddles (budon pialan)¶

    1. The sons, the chieftaincy and the children (?)¶

    Poaa lebooloa nda ŋiɔn (tale, p. 168)

    1. Piɔɔ sia o sia le, kpɛɛnɛi kon nɔ mɛnndan lɔ́ɔ́-lɔ́ɔ̀. — mulio: Lembulten
    1979: Piöö sia o sia le, kpèènèi kon nö mènndan löölöö. ( Mulio : Lombulten )
    “Rain or no rain, this pot always has water.” — The lemon. (Keita 1979, p. 168)
    Handwritten notes: Halaa above Piöö; “the rain rains or does not, this pot always has water”; “dumbullen = lemon”. Answer typed Lombulten; the 1979 glossary has Lembulten.
    2. Cɛi kala ni pɔmbɔ kandun yindɔɔ o lebol. — mulio: Hollo
    1979: Tyièi kala ni pömbö kandun yindöö o lebol. ( Mulio : Hollo )
    “Little mother's (the aunt's) house starts its roof at the neck.” — The mushroom. (Keita 1979, p. 168)
    Handwritten note: “the house of the little mother (aunt) starts with the head — mushroom”.

    2. Sofɛlɛ and his master¶

    Sofɛlɛ nda masanɔ ndɔɔ (tale, p. 169)

    3. Cua tinda kɛndɔɔ ho o bɔɔ nin te. — mulio: Kɛwvo
    1979: Tyua tinda kèndöö ho o böö nin te. ( Mulio : Kèwvo )
    “Do not pick up this fine stick in the yard!” — The snake. (Keita 1979, p. 169)
    4. Kɔnndan tan tiɔn, kumbinndo lɔ́ɔ́-lɔ́ɔ̀ diompilɛ. — mulio: Hɔlta koongolan
    1979: Könndan tan tiön, kumbinndo löölöö diompilè. ( Mulio : Hölta koongolan )
    “These two holes, always open … (?).” — The eye sockets. (Keita 1979, p. 169)

    3. Vɛɛn and jealousy (?)¶

    Vɛɛn paŋalaa (tale, pp. 170–171)

    5. Peele kɛndɛlen le coya o hɔl, kɛ i tiwba le len cua le. — mulio: Vana coe yema nuu o nɔw nin te.
    1979: Peele kèndèlen le tyoya, o höl, kè i tiwba le len tyua le ( Mulio : Vana tyoe yema nuu o nöw nin te )
    “Beautiful leaves are before me, but I cannot take this one.” — The one who is my sister: no marriage with her. (Keita 1979, p. 171)
    Handwritten notes: “women” above Peele; “in front of me, I cannot take it”; “[?] marriage”.
