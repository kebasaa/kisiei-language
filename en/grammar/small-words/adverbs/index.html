    Adverbs and ideophones¶

    Adverbs are invariable; they are usually placed after the verb and its objects, at the end of the sentence or just before the negation (Keita 1979, p. 116). The forms and examples below are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG).

    Adverbs of time¶

    Word Meaning Example (Bible)
    hau today, now kandɔɔ hau “from now on” Gen 4:11
    nihau now, this time Má cè nihau paa “This at last is bone” Gen 2:23
    sunsun now, right away Lelan sunsun “Now therefore” Gen 21:23
    siɔdii tomorrow Siɔdii idii “tomorrow morning” Gen 19:2
    idii, idiiyo in the morning, early Diimandɔɔ idiiyo púŋ “early the next morning” Gen 21:14
    icɔllo / ipalaa evening / morning, day Mí ɲɔɔ yɔŋnun icɔllo a ipalaa “There was evening and there was morning” Gen 1:5
    ɲinasiaŋ next year boondei he ɲinasiaŋ-ndo “at this time next year” Gen 17:21
    le fɛɛn a fɛɛn for ever o co vannɔn yoomu le fɛɛn a fɛɛn “he would live for ever” Gen 3:22
    few never (with the negation) Ɲɛm mun pɛnginin tosanndo le few “these things will never cease” Gen 8:22

    Adverbs of place¶

    Word Meaning Example (Bible)
    nanu here muungu nanu “will come back here” Gen 15:16
    lon there, over there má cal lon “they settled there” Gen 11:31
    lecoo on top, above ɲɛ-ɲɛ ve lecoo le “there was nothing on it” Gen 1:2
    labɛngu underneath, below 1 Kgs 7:34

    Adverbs of quantity and degree¶

    Word Meaning Example (Bible)
    taw very, much n naŋ taw “it was very good” Gen 1:31
    kpow completely, all Mɛɛ Hala cii baala kon tosa kpow Gen 2:2
    faw all, completely visia bɛndua a à pɔmbɔa faw Gen 8:17

    See also the indefinites.

    Ideophones¶

    Ideophones are expressive words that depict a sound, a manner, an intensity or a visual impression; they follow the verb or adjective they reinforce and are often reduplicated. They form a class of their own, with a particular prosody, described in detail for Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 132-140). Examples from the Bible:

    • idiiyo púŋ “early in the morning, at dawn” Gen 21:14;
    • mà faŋan … few-few “go away at once!” Gen 12:19;
    • hoeyii ken niinu … kpɛŋ “these locusts covered (the land) entirely” Exod 10:13;
    • o puiyaa tɛi-tɛi “well watered everywhere” Gen 13:10.

    Spelling rules

    • Adverbs are written as separate words: kandɔɔ hau, n naŋ taw.
    • A reduplicated ideophone is written with a hyphen: few-few, tɛi-tɛi; the Bible often marks the tone on it (púŋ).
    • Fixed expressions are written as separate words: le fɛɛn a fɛɛn.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 description gives lists of adverbs of place, time, manner and quantity in the old orthography (kpèèlun, levil, nanu, lon, lebèngu; lapöm, haw, siödii, diilèn, sunsun; taw, faw, tyapo…) (Keita 1979, p. 116). Most of them are found in the Bible in their current form (nanu, lon, hau, sunsun, diilɛn, taw, faw).
