    The complex sentence¶

    The rules on this page are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG). The conjunctions themselves are presented on the Prepositions, postpositions and conjunctions page.

    Narrative sequence: mí, mó, má¶

    Narrative links clauses together with mí + pronoun (high tone): Mí Hala dimi aa … Mí ɲɔɔ tosan lende “And God said… And it was so” Gen 1:6-7. Each new action is a clause introduced by mí, mó or má; see Tense, aspect, mood.

    Purpose and consequence¶

    Construction Meaning Example
    mì / mò / mà + verb so that, and may N cɔwnun vanacieo a velan naa, mò vel naa “Let us make man in our image, so that he resembles us” Gen 1:26
    haliko mi … in order that haliko mi la va baloo dɔwnɔnndo “so that it may be your food” Gen 1:29
    le + long form in order to (do) le dioo “to eat” Gen 3:11

    Cause¶

    kani “for, because” and le tùú “because” introduce the cause: kani o falun vanapɔnɔɔ nin “for she was taken out of man” Gen 2:23; Le tùú n cɛl sɔɛi laa nɔm-nde “because you listened to the voice of your wife” Gen 3:17. lelan and le hee “that is why” introduce the consequence Gen 2:24.

    Condition: te … pɛ¶

    The condition opens with te and closes with pɛ, placed just after the verb of the condition; the consequence follows:

    • te o de pɛ kɔmnde yɔm kon, o co vannɔn yoomu le fɛɛn a fɛɛn “if he eats of the fruit of this tree, he will live for ever” Gen 3:22;
    • unreal condition with the lengthened pronoun: te yáà de pɛ visin “if I had eaten meat” Lev 10:19.

    A negative condition keeps pɛ and places the negation at the end of the condition: Kɛ taa liila ya le, i kuenin te “but if you do not come with me, I will not go” Judg 4:8 (taa = te + a “if you”).

    Time¶

    Mɛɛ “when” and Tuma “at the time when” open a time clause, often closed by a class referent: Tuma Mɛlɛka Hala cɔw halataalaa a lɛŋnden-ndo “When the Lord God made the heavens and the earth” Gen 2:4; Mɛɛ Hala cii baala kon tosa kpow, mó mal vallo “When God had finished his work, he rested” Gen 2:2.

    Complement clauses¶

    After verbs of saying, seeing, knowing and wanting, the complement clause is introduced by maa: mó ca maa n naŋ taw “he saw that it was very good” Gen 1:31; I sina nihau válán maa a nɔ siòòŋii Hala-e “now I know that you fear God” Gen 22:12.

    Relative clauses¶

    See Relative clauses.

    Spelling rules

    • A comma separates the condition or the time clause from the main clause: te o de pɛ kɔmnde yɔm kon, …
    • taa (“if you”) is written as one word, as in the Bible.
