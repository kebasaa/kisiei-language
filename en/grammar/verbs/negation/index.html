    Negation¶

    Negation is marked by a particle at the end of the clause, le or te, and, for many verbs, by a change of stem (Keita 1979, p. 72, 94-104). The same structure is described for Southern Kissi: a final particle le and changes of tone and vowel on the verb (Childs 1995, p. 222-223, 260-264). The rules and examples below are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG).

    The final particle: le or te¶

    The particle is placed at the end of the negated clause, after the objects and complements:

    • I sínà le “I do not know” 1 Sam 17:55;
    • o di ɲɛdiaa le, o kol vɛlɛ le “he ate no food and did not drink either” Exod 34:28.

    It takes the form te when the word before it ends in a nasal consonant (n, m, ŋ) or in l; after a vowel or w, it stays le. In the Bible, te follows a nasal more than 2,600 times and l more than 360 times, and le is never found after these consonants. The 1979 description mentions only the nasals (Keita 1979, p. 72); the description of Southern Kissi gives te after any consonant (Childs 1995, p. 142-143).

    • I mel nɔm te “I will not let you (go)” Gen 32:27;
    • hali pilanndo bɛɛ la pilan te “do not even touch it” Gen 3:3;
    • i kuenin te “I will not go” Judg 4:8;
    • n fúlà lɛ ndu o hɔl te “you shall not appear before him” (after l) Gen 43:5;
    • ndóò ci lɛ taw le “he could no longer see at all” (after w) Gen 48:10.

    Spelling rule

    • Write te after n, m, ŋ or l, and le after a vowel or w: I mel nɔm te, o hɔl te, but o kol vɛlɛ le, taw le.
    • The particle is always written as a separate word, at the end of the negated clause, before the comma or full stop.

    The verb in the negative¶

    Negative stem¶

    Alternating verbs take a stem with a changed vowel in the negative (see Stems and groups):

    Affirmative Negative
    a mam “you laughed” I mem te “I did not laugh” Gen 18:15
    mal “leave” I mel nɔm te Gen 32:27
    de “eat” o di ɲɛdiaa le Exod 34:28
    kɔa “go” te naa n kue … le Gen 43:3

    Progressive and “to be” in the past: ve¶

    In the negative, the past auxiliary va becomes ve: ɲɛ-ɲɛ ve lecoo le “there was nothing on it” Gen 1:2; vanaa … ve cɛinin te “the people were not in the house” Gen 39:11. Southern Kissi has the same wa / we alternation (Childs 1995, p. 234).

    Future: -nin … le / te¶

    The negative future keeps the suffix -nin and, for alternating verbs, the vowel of the negative: o dinin ndu tɔnɔ le “he will not eat of it (its fruit)” Deut 28:30; i kɛɛnin te “I will not refuse” 1 Kgs 2:20.

    Prohibition (negative imperative)¶

    The negative imperative uses the habitual stem and the final particle: La dia le “do not eat of it” Gen 3:3, La suunda le “You shall not commit adultery” Exod 20:14.

    “Not yet”, “nothing”, “nobody”¶

    • vɔ … le “not yet”: Mɛlɛka Hala ndóò tuisi vɔ piɔɔ … le Gen 2:5;
    • ɲɛ-ɲɛ … le “nothing”: ɲɛ-ɲɛ ve lecoo le Gen 1:2;
    • vana-vana … le “nobody”: vana-vana ve lasiɛmbu … le Gen 2:25.

    Negating an identity¶

    To negate “it is X”, co and the final particle are used, where the affirmative uses the particle ni: Hoo co masa Yuifia ni “this is the king of the Jews” Luke 23:38 versus O co dɛnɛ vana bɛnda le tosa le “it is not a thing that ought to be done” Gen 20:9. In this construction, Southern Kissi puts the noun's class pronoun before it (Childs 1995, p. 261-262); this point still needs to be checked for the Kisiei of Guinea.

    1979 usage

    The le / te rule is described in 1979 for the nasals only (“le is realised as te when the final sound of the word before it is nasal”) (Keita 1979, p. 72); the Bible extends it to l. The 1979 tables also give the negative of each tense (I tyo kolö le, I kol te, I kolnin te) (Keita 1979, p. 96-104).
