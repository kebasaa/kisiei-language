    Verb stems and groups¶

    Long form and short forms¶

    Every verb has a long form (called the “infinitive” in 1979 (Keita 1979, p. 90-91)), ending in a long vowel or a class ending, and one or more short forms (stems):

    Long form Short form Meaning
    tosaa tosa do, make
    malɔɔ mal leave, let go
    kolɔɔ kol drink
    dioo de, dia, dio, di eat
    kɔlan kɔa, kɔɛ, kɔ, kue go, leave
    cɛlɔɔ cɛl accept

    The long form is used after the auxiliaries co and va (progressive aspect) and as a verbal noun: o va kelaa “he was walking” Gen 6:9; dɛnɛ vaa nuaa tosaa “to do such a thing” Gen 11:6. The short forms are used for the other tenses and moods and for the negative (see Tense, aspect, mood).

    The long form is in fact a verbal noun of the o class; it ends like a noun (-aa, -ɔɔ, -oo, -ndo…). Southern Kissi likewise forms the verbal noun with the suffix -o (Childs 1995, p. 202-203).

    Regular verbs and alternating verbs¶

    The 1979 description sorts verbs into four groups according to the number of different stems (Keita 1979, p. 101-104). For Southern Kissi, a simpler distinction is made between regular verbs, whose vowel does not change, and irregular verbs, whose vowel changes with tense, aspect and polarity; tense is marked first of all by tone (Childs 1995, p. 219-224). The texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG) confirm these two types:

    Regular verbs (1st group of 1979)¶

    The vowel stays the same throughout; only the ending changes:

    Long form Perfective Future Negative
    tosaa tosa tosanin tosa le
    cɛlɔɔ cɛl cɛlnin

    Examples: Mí ɲɔɔ tosan lende “And it was so” Gen 1:7; ɲɛ-ɲɛ nɔla lɛ mò kuuna nda … tosa le “nothing will stop them from doing…” Gen 11:6.

    Vowel-alternating verbs (2nd to 4th group of 1979)¶

    The stem vowel changes, above all in the negative:

    Meaning Affirmative Negative Example (Bible)
    leave mal mel I mel nɔm te “I will not let you go” Gen 32:27
    laugh mam mem I mem te “I did not laugh”; a mam “you laughed” Gen 18:15
    eat de, dia di o di ɲɛdiaa le “he did not eat” Exod 34:28
    go kɔa, kɔɛ kue te naa n kue … le “if we do not go” Gen 43:3

    These changes follow a few regular patterns, found across all the verbs in the lexicon:

    Change Examples (affirmative / negative)
    a → e mam / mem, mal / mel, ca / ce
    u → i vu / vi, hun / hin
    o → u tol / tul, kow / kuw
    io, ia → i, e hiow / hiw, dia / di, pial / pel
    ɔa → ue kɔa / kue

    Example: Taa yeema mà vi le “if you do not want to die” Gen 19:17 (vu “die”). The description of Southern Kissi reports the same kind of vowel alternation in its irregular verbs (Childs 1995, p. 220-221, 241-244).

    The negative future keeps the vowel of the negative: dinin “will not eat” Deut 28:30, kuenin te “I will not go” Judg 4:8.

    The forms of “eat” and “go”¶

    The most irregular verbs have four stems. Their use in the Bible:

    eat go Use Example
    long form dioo kɔlan progressive, verbal noun le dioo “to eat”
    perfective, subordinate de kɔa narrative, condition, relative te o de pɛ “if he eats” Gen 3:22; má kɔa yondoo nin “they went to the fields” Gen 4:8
    habitual, prohibition dia kɔɛ habit, negative imperative, subjunctive La dia le “do not eat of it” Gen 3:3; mì n kɔɛ “let us go” Gen 4:8
    short stem dio kɔ near future, serial verb n co ndu dio “you will eat of it” Gen 2:17; N kɔ delnun ndu coo “let us go and fall upon him” Gen 37:20
    negative di kue negation o di ɲɛdiaa le Exod 34:28
    future dianin kɔanin future nda dianin “they will eat” Exod 12:7

    Spelling rules

    • Write each stem as it is pronounced: mal / mel, dia / di; do not “regularise” it.
    • The future suffix -nin is written joined to the stem: tosanin, dianin, kuenin.
    • The long form is written with its long vowel or its ending: tosaa, malɔɔ, dioo.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 description names the stems R1 to R4 and gives, for each group, a table of their use (Keita 1979, p. 101-104), as well as a lexicon of verbs sorted by group (Keita 1979, p. 110-118). These tables are in the old orthography (Dioo : de, dia, dio, di; Kölan : köè, köa, kö, koe). The distribution of stems found in the Bible broadly agrees, but the Bible writes kɔa for the narrative perfective and kɔɛ after mì, where 1979 gives köè for the past and köa for the habitual; this point still needs to be checked with speakers.
