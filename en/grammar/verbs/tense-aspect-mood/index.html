    Tense, aspect and mood¶

    The Kisiei verb is not conjugated for person: the subject is carried by the pronoun. Tense, aspect and mood are expressed by:

    1. the tone of the verb and of the pronoun (rarely marked in writing);
    2. the choice of stem (long or short form, changed vowel; see Stems and groups);
    3. auxiliaries placed before the verb (co, va);
    4. the suffix -nin;
    5. lengthening of the subject pronoun (ndóò, yáà).

    Aspect matters more than tense: the first contrast is between the completed action (perfective) and the ongoing or habitual action (Childs 1995, p. 219-222). The forms and examples on this page are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG); they are compared with the 1979 description (Keita 1979, p. 94-101) and with that of Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 218-245).

    Overview¶

    Form Construction Example (Bible)
    Perfective pronoun + short stem I tuei “I have heard” Gen 27:6
    Habitual pronoun + stem (different tone) o co kɛndɛ “he is well” Gen 29:6
    Present progressive co + long form i co hunɔɔ “I am coming, I am going to” Gen 6:17
    Past progressive va + long form o va kelaa “he was walking” Gen 6:9
    Near future co + short stem n co ndu dio “you will eat of it” Gen 2:17
    Future stem + -nin I tuisinin nɔm “I will put on you” Gen 3:16
    Remote past lengthened pronoun + stem Kɛwvo ndóò hiow “the serpent was more (cunning)” Gen 3:1
    Future in the past, conditional lengthened pronoun + stem-nin mɛɛ ndóò kenin nda diolan yɛ “what he would call them” Gen 2:19
    Narrative (“and then…”) mí / mó / má + stem Mí Hala dimi aa “And God said” Gen 1:3
    Subjunctive mì / mò / mà + stem Yondoo fulá mò kumbu kɔngɔ vesɛiyo “let the vegetation come forth and cover the earth” Gen 1:11
    Imperative stem (+ la in the plural) La náŋ ipúlú, la kuí “Be fruitful, multiply” Gen 1:28

    Perfective and habitual¶

    The short form of the verb, without an auxiliary, expresses the completed action: I tuei “I have heard”, a mam “you laughed” Gen 18:15. The same form also expresses habit or a general truth; the two are distinguished by tone (low tone for the habitual, high tone for the perfective) (Keita 1979, p. 95), which the description of Southern Kissi confirms (Childs 1995, p. 224-227). The Bible does not mark this tone: the context decides.

    The progressive: co and va¶

    The auxiliary co “to be (in the act of)” followed by the long form expresses an action in progress; in the past, va is used:

    • i co hunɔɔ mɛmma bɛnduan soli “I am going to bring the flood” Gen 6:17;
    • Kɔl Hala-o va o pɛɛngaa o mɛmma man coo “the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters” Gen 1:2.

    co and va are also the forms of the verb to be: Lɛŋnden le va tulumasaa “the earth was formless” Gen 1:2. In the negative, va becomes ve: ɲɛ-ɲɛ ve lecoo le “there was nothing on it” Gen 1:2. Southern Kissi has the same wa / we contrast (Childs 1995, p. 233-234).

    The future¶

    Two constructions exist side by side:

    1. co + short stem (near or certain future): n co ndu dio “(the day) you eat of it” Gen 2:17. This is the ordinary future construction in Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 234-235); the 1979 description calls it the futur immédiat (immediate future) (Keita 1979, p. 95).
    2. stem + -nin (future, promise, prediction; futur lointain, distant future, in 1979): I tuisinin nɔm sangalan coo “I will multiply your pains” Gen 3:16, I yɔngunin cuavaa nɔmaa imamaa lɛŋnden nden “I will give this land to your offspring” Gen 12:7.

    In the negative, -nin keeps the vowel of the negative: i kuenin te “I will not go” Judg 4:8, i kɛɛnin te “I will not refuse” 1 Kgs 2:20.

    The lengthened pronoun: remote past and conditional¶

    Lengthening the subject pronoun, with a high tone, places the action or state in a remote past: “he was (once)”, “he had”. Southern Kissi has exactly the same device (óó kòl, ndóó) (Childs 1995, p. 231-232). The Bible writes the lengthened independent pronoun as one word, with its tones:

    Person Form Example
    1st sing. yáà te yáà de pɛ “if I had eaten” Lev 10:19
    3rd sing. ndóò yɔmndo ndu pilɛ bɛɛ ndóò vil-lɔ … le “no tree had yet sprung up” Gen 2:5
    3rd plur. ndáà ɲɛm vanaa kan ndáà manda “the provisions these people had kept” Gen 14:11

    With -nin, the same form expresses the future in the past or the conditional: mɛɛ ndóò kenin nda diolan yɛ “what he would call them” Gen 2:19; ndóò venin a siɛmbula lan le “would she not be covered with shame?” Num 12:14.

    The 1979 description classes these forms under a conditional mood marked by lengthening of the pronoun (Ii kolnin, Ii va kolöö) (Keita 1979, p. 99-100).

    Narrative and subjunctive: mi + pronoun¶

    The word mi “and (then)” combines with the subject pronoun: mó (mi + o), má (mi + a), mí n, mí la. The tone of this form is grammatical, and the Bible marks it:

    • high tone: completed action, the sequence of the narrative: mó yaa nda “and he brought them” Gen 2:19, má kuumbuu “and they covered” Gen 9:23;
    • low tone: non-completed action: purpose, wish, expected consequence (subjunctive): mò vel naa “(that he may be) in our likeness” Gen 1:26, mì n kɔɛ “so that we may go” Gen 4:8.

    Southern Kissi has the same forms (mbo = mi + o, ma, mila, miŋ) and the same tone contrast (mbó kwɛ̀ “then he left”, mbò kwɛ̀ “then he leaves”) (Childs 1995, p. 239-240). In 1979, the subjunctive is described with maa + pronoun (Keita 1979, p. 100); the Bible uses maa “that” after verbs of saying and knowing (mó ca maa n naŋ taw “he saw that it was very good” Gen 1:31).

    Spelling rule

    Write the tone on mi + pronoun, as the Bible does: mí, mó, má (narrative) versus mì, mò, mà (subjunctive). This is one of the rare cases where the tone mark is obligatory; see Tone.

    The imperative and the hortative¶

    • 2nd person singular: the bare stem: Kɔ “go”, Cua ndu “take her” Gen 12:19.
    • 2nd person plural: la + stem: La náŋ ipúlú, la kuí Gen 1:28.
    • 1st person plural (“let us go”): n + stem: N kɔ delnun ndu coo Gen 37:20.
    • 3rd person (“let him…”): subject + stem with high tone, which the Bible often marks: Ɲɔ́ɔ́ téngá “Let there be light” Gen 1:3, Yondoo fulá “let the vegetation come forth” Gen 1:11.

    Southern Kissi likewise distinguishes the imperative and the hortative by tone (Childs 1995, p. 227-231).

    “Not yet”, “already”¶

    • vɔ … le “not yet”: Mɛlɛka Hala ndóò tuisi vɔ piɔɔ … le “the Lord God had not yet sent rain” Gen 2:5 (Southern Kissi wɔ̀ (Childs 1995, p. 238-239));
    • bɛɛ … le “not yet, not even”: O cii bɛɛ piɛilen tosa le “he had not yet finished speaking” Gen 24:15;
    • cii “finish” + verb expresses the completed action: Mɛɛ Hala cii baala kon tosa kpow “when God had finished his work” Gen 2:2.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 description gives conjugation tables by tense (durative present with tyo, habitual present, past, near and distant future, imperfect with va, imperative, conditional, subjunctive) (Keita 1979, p. 94-104). The auxiliary tyo is written co in the Bible. The tone distinctions between habitual present and past are not marked there; they still need to be described with speakers (see the open questions).
