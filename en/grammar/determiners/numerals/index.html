    Numbers¶

    The forms and rules on this page are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG), which contains a great many numbers (ages, censuses, dates). They are compared with the 1979 arithmetic lessons (Keita 1979, p. 16-63) and with the description of Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 112-114).

    Cardinal numbers¶

    From 1 to 10¶
    1 pilɛ 6 ŋɔmpum
    2 tiɔn 7 ŋɔmmɛw
    3 yáá 8 ŋɔmaa
    4 hiɔɔlu 9 ŋɔmahiɔɔlu
    5 ŋuɛɛnu 10 tɔ́

    The numbers from 6 to 9 are built on 5: ŋɔm- + 1, 2, 3, 4 (ŋɔmpum, ŋɔmmɛw, ŋɔmaa, ŋɔmahiɔɔlu).

    Tens, hundreds, thousands¶
    20 bidiin 70 belaŋɔmmɛw
    30 beleyaa 80 belaŋɔmaa
    40 belehiɔɔlu 90 belaŋɔmahiɔɔlu
    50 beleŋuɛɛnu 100 kɛmɛ
    60 belaŋɔmpum 1000 vaa (plur. valan)

    The words for 100 and 1000 are loanwords from Manding (Childs 1995, p. 112). The tens from 30 to 90 are formed with bele- / bela- + the unit. The hundreds are formed with le-: kɛmɛ lediin “200” Gen 11:19, kɛmɛ leyáá “300” Gen 5:22, kɛmɛ leŋɔmpum “600” Gen 7:6. Thousands are counted with the plural valan: vàálan tan ŋɔmmɛw a kɛmɛ leŋuɛɛnu “7,500” Num 3:22.

    Compound numbers¶

    The parts of a compound number are linked by a “and”, from the largest to the smallest: kɛmɛ a beleyaa “130” Gen 5:3, bidiin a laŋɔmahiɔɔlu “29” Gen 11:24.

    The last element takes a prefix agreeing with the class of the noun counted, as in Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 112-113):

    Noun counted Prefix Example
    lan (vɔsilan “years”) la- vɔsilan kɛmɛ leŋɔmahiɔɔlu a tɔ́ a latiɔn “912 years” Gen 5:8
    persons, animals à visia tɔ́ a à hiɔɔlu “14 animals” Gen 7:2
    len (ordinal) le- paale bidiin a leŋɔmmɛwndɔle “the 27th day” Gen 8:14

    Spelling rules

    • The elements of a compound number are written separately, linked by a: kɛmɛ a beleyaa.
    • The agreement prefix is written attached to the unit: latiɔn, laŋuɛɛnu; the prefix for persons à is written separately, with its tone.
    • In running text, large numbers may be written in figures, in brackets after the form in words, as the Bible does: “(7 500)”.

    Position of the number and agreement¶

    The number is placed after the noun. From 2 upwards, it is often preceded by the class pronoun of the noun (object form; see Personal pronouns):

    Class Example Meaning
    plural persons simlacia nda yáá three warriors 1 Chr 11:15
    i plur. palɛi ndii yáá three days 1 Chr 12:40
    plur. in -on Paangon mún yáá three months 1 Chr 13:14
    an polan man yáá three rows 1 Kgs 6:36
    lan kundala bɛndulan tan tiɔn two great (lights) Gen 1:6

    Without a pronoun, the number follows the noun directly: kɔla palɛi yaa “a three days’ journey” Exod 3:18, vanaa yaa “three people”. The pronoun of the lan class takes the form tan after n (vàálan tan).

    “Two” changes form according to the class of the noun counted: ŋiɔn for persons (poaa nda ŋiɔn “two sons” Gen 10:25), ŋiin for the i class (palɛi ndii ŋiin “two days” Exod 16:29), miɔn for the an class (polan man miɔn “two rows” 1 Kgs 7:18) and tiɔn for the lan class. Southern Kissi has the same series (ŋìɔ́ɔ́ŋ, ŋìíŋ, mìɔ̀ɔ́ŋ, tìɔ́ɔ́ŋ), whereas “three” varies little there and “four” not at all (Childs 1995, p. 113). The class pronoun attaches there to the number (sálà-láŋ là-tìɔ́ɔ́ŋ “two sacrifices”) (Childs 1995, p. 113); in the Bible, it attaches only to the unit of a compound number (latiɔn).

    Ordinal numbers¶

    “First” is tasoo Gen 1:1. The other ordinals are formed with -ndɔɔ and agree with the class of the noun:

    Ordinal o class i class len class
    second diinndɔɔ diinndɔɛi
    third yàándɔɔ
    fourth hiɔɔlundɔɔ
    seventh ŋɔmmɛwndɔɔ ŋɔmmɛwndɔlen
    tenth tɔ́ndɔɔ tɔ́ndɔɛi

    Examples: paale ŋɔmmɛwndɔlen “the seventh day” Gen 2:2, paangii tɔ́ndɔɛi “the tenth month” Gen 8:5, paangii diinndɔɛi “the second month” Gen 8:14.

    “Second” is diinndɔɔ; tiɔnndɔɔ is very rare Neh 13:6. The stem diin “two” is also found in bidiin “20” and kɛmɛ lediin “200”; Southern Kissi likewise forms “second” on dìíŋ and says “first” with a separate word, tásè (Childs 1995, p. 112, 201).

    Number of times¶

    The prefix le- forms adverbs of frequency: leyaa “three times”, leŋɔmmɛw “seven times” (Keita 1979, p. 35). See Derivation.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 arithmetic lessons give the same numbers in the old spelling (Pilè, Tiön, Yaa, Hiöölu, Nhuèènu…, Kèmè lediin, Valan la tiön) (Keita 1979, p. 16-63). The Bible writes tiɔn, ŋuɛɛnu, kɛmɛ, and links the class pronoun to the thousands in the form vàálan tan.
