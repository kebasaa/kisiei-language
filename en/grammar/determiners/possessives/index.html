    Possessives¶

    The possessive is placed after the noun, which takes its stem form: cɛi ndɔɔ “his house”, diola ndɔlan “his name”. It varies according to:

    • the person of the possessor (me, you, him/her, us, you, them);
    • the class and number of the noun possessed;
    • and, in the o class, according to whether what is possessed is a person or a thing.

    The construction is the same in Southern Kissi: the noun possessed, followed by its class pronoun (except in the o class), then the possessive carrying the suffix of the noun (Childs 1995, p. 106).

    Possessed noun of the o class: persons and things¶

    The Bible (BKG) clearly distinguishes two series. With a noun for a person (relative, master, servant), the possessive is short; with a noun for a thing, it carries the class suffix -ndo / -ɔ:

    Possessor Person possessed Thing possessed
    1st sing. fuɲa nuu “my father” Gen 20:13 kɔl nuu “my heart” Gen 6:13
    2nd sing. fuɲa nɔm “your father” Gen 12:1 kɔl nɔmndo “your heart” Josh 14:9
    3rd sing. fuɲa ndɔ “his father” Gen 2:24 cɛi ndɔɔ “his house” Gen 39:4
    1st plur. fuɲa naa “our father” Gen 19:34 cɛi naa “our house” Song 1:17
    2nd plur. fuɲa niaa “your father” Gen 27:6 yoomu niaa “your life” Gen 9:5
    3rd plur. fuɲa nda “their father” Gen 9:23 cɛi ndaa “their house” Neh 3:23

    The short forms nɔm, ndɔ, nda are those of the object pronouns. A few nouns are used with both series depending on the meaning: po ndɔ “his son” (172 times) but po ndɔɔ (67 times).

    Rule

    For a relative or a person: kala ndɔ “his mother” Gen 2:24, laa nɔm “your wife” Gen 3:17. For a thing: fonda ndɔɔ “his place” Gen 36:33, kɔl nɔmndo “your heart”.

    General table (Bible)¶

    Possessor o (thing) plural persons i plur. -on an len lan
    1st sing. nuu nia nii nian nilen nilan
    2nd sing. nɔmndo nɔmnda nɔmnde nɔmndon nɔmndan nɔmnden nɔmndan
    3rd sing. ndɔɔ ndɔa ndɔɛi ndɔn ndɔan ndɔlen ndɔlan
    1st plur. naa naa naan naalen naalan
    2nd plur. niaa niaa niɛi niaan niaalen niaalan
    3rd plur. ndaa ndaa ndɛi ndan ndan ndalen ndalan

    Examples:

    • i: sɔɛi nii “my word” Gen 22:18, sɔɛi ndɛi “their language” Gen 11:7;
    • -on: vanayan nɔmndon “your kindred” Gen 12:1;
    • an: yoomuma nian “my strength” Gen 31:6, kooma ndɔan “its blood” Exod 12:7;
    • len: lɛŋnde nilen “my land” Gen 20:15, lɛŋnde naalen “our land” Gen 26:16;
    • lan: silala nilan “my laws” Gen 26:5, diola ndalan “their names” Gen 10:32;
    • plural persons: vanaa nia bɛndua “my lords” Gen 18:3, poaa ndɔa “his sons” Gen 7:7, cuavaa naa “our children” Gen 31:16.

    In Southern Kissi too, the 1st person singular possessive has two forms, nù and nì, like nuu and ni- (nii, nilen) in the Bible; the possessives there have a low tone, whereas the object pronouns have a high tone (Childs 1995, p. 106).

    1st sing. with plural persons, and 2nd plur.

    The Bible distinguishes nia “my” (with plural persons) and niaa “your” (plural possessor): vanaa nia “my lords”, fuɲaa niaa “your fathers”. The 1979 spelling writes both as niaa (Keita 1979, p. 61).

    Possessive pronouns¶

    To say “mine, yours…”, the possessive is preceded by the class pronoun (Keita 1979, p. 60-62): o nuu “mine” (o class), i nii (i class), le nilen (len class), la nilan (lan class), ma nian (an class).

    Other ways of expressing possession¶

    Two other constructions are found (Keita 1979, p. 62-63):

    1. with the verb nɔw “have” and the particle ni: Tamba nɔ cɛiyo hoo ni “Tamba has this house”;
    2. with the class referent and ni: Naw Fatee vo ni “It is Fatee’s cow”.

    In the Bible, a named possessor is placed after the noun possessed, followed by the class referent: lɛŋnde Esipte len “the land of Egypt” Gen 13:10, nakɔ Mɛlɛka-o “the garden of the Lord” Gen 13:10.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 spelling writes the 2nd person num- (nummdo, nummde) (Keita 1979, p. 61); the Bible writes nɔm-: nɔmndo, nɔmnde. The 1979 forms of the 1st and 2nd plur. for the len and lan classes (nialen, nialan) are niaalen, niaalan in the Bible.
