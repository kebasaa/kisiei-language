    Demonstratives¶

    Demonstratives point to a being or a thing. Kisiei has two series of them: one for what is near (“this”), the other for what is far or already mentioned (“that”). Like the other determiners, they agree with the class of the noun (see Agreement).

    Table¶

    Class of the noun Near: adjective Near: pronoun Far Example (Bible)
    o / a / ɔ sing. ho hoo kon kobio ho “this city” Gen 18:24; kobi kon “that city” Gen 4:17
    plural persons ha haa kan vannda ha “these people” Gen 7:1; vanaa kan “those people” Gen 10:5
    i he hee ken pembei he “this mountain” Exod 3:12; pembii ken “that mountain” Gen 22:14
    an man man man kooma man “this blood” Exod 12:13
    len len len len paale len “that day” Gen 30:35
    lan lan lan lan kobila lan “these cities” Gen 19:25
    plural in -on / -n mun mun mun sɔɔn mun “these words, these things” Gen 22:1; ɲɛm mun “these things” Gen 21:14

    Form of the noun before the demonstrative¶

    The texts of the Bible (BKG) confirm the rule described in 1979 (Keita 1979, p. 57):

    • with the near demonstrative, the noun keeps its full form: kobio ho, vannda ha, pembei he;
    • with the far demonstrative, the noun takes its stem form: kobi kon, vanaa kan.

    Southern Kissi has the same distribution: the near demonstrative follows the full form of the noun, the far one follows the stem (accompanied by the class pronoun outside the o class) (Childs 1995, p. 110-111).

    In the i class, the stem form before ken replaces -ei with -ii: pembei he / pembii ken “this/that mountain”, paangei he / paangii ken “this/that month” Exod 12:3 Gen 8:5.

    In the an, len and lan classes, the noun is always in the stem form + class pronoun (mɛmma man, lɛŋnde len, kobila lan; see Noun classes). Near and far are distinguished there by tone (Keita 1979, p. 58), which the Bible does not mark. In Southern Kissi, the near demonstrative carries a falling tone and the far one a high tone (Childs 1995, p. 110).

    Spelling rule

    • Near adjective: ho, ha, he (a single vowel): kobio ho.
    • Near pronoun, used on its own: hoo, haa, hee (double vowel): Hoo co “this one is…” Luke 23:38.
    • The demonstrative is always written separately from the noun.

    Uses of the pronoun¶

    The demonstrative used on its own replaces a noun:

    • Hoo co masa Yuifia ni “This is the king of the Jews” Luke 23:38;
    • Le hee “because of this, that is why”, very frequent at the start of a sentence Gen 2:21;
    • O kon kɔɔli “after that” (o class), a narrative formula: O sɔɔn mun kɔɔli “after these events” Gen 22:1.

    1979 usage

    In 1979, ho and hoo are given as a single form, and tan, ten as variants of lan, len after l (Keita 1979, p. 57-58). The Bible distinguishes ho (adjective) and hoo (pronoun) and does not use tan, ten as demonstratives. The description of Southern Kissi also gives a single form for the adjective and the pronoun (hóò) (Childs 1995, p. 110).
