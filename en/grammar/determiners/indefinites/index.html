    Indefinites¶

    Indefinites refer to beings and things in a vague way: “a certain”, “other”, “all”, “each”, “anyone”. Like the other determiners, they are placed after the noun (Keita 1979, p. 71-73). The forms below are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG).

    Invariable¶

    Word Meaning Example (Bible)
    pum a certain, a Pee bɛndoo pum “a great river” Gen 2:10; Telen pum hiovɔɔ “some time passed” Gen 4:3
    celen other, another po celen “another son” Gen 4:25; cuavaa celen “other children” Gen 5:4
    tɔ̀a the others vanaa tɔ̀a “the other people” Gen 4:15
    kpow all, whole o lɛŋnden kpow coo “on the whole earth” Gen 1:11; súúlan kpow “all kinds” Gen 1:21
    kpede all (without exception) yowva kpede “all the birds” Gen 8:17
    faw all, entirely visia bɛndua a à pɔmbɔa faw “the animals great and small, all of them” Gen 8:17

    celen varies neither in class nor in number (po celen, cuavaa celen). pum corresponds to the Southern Kissi adjective pùm “some” (Childs 1995, p. 127).

    “Each”, “any”: X oo X¶

    A repeated noun, linked by oo, means “each, every”:

    • bil oo bil “every plant”; yɔm oo yɔm “every tree” Gen 1:29;
    • Visi oo visi “each animal” Gen 2:19;
    • fonda oo fonda “everywhere, in every place” Gen 20:13.

    The noun is in the stem form; oo is written separately. Several time nouns take a prefix i- and other nouns the class pronoun le-: idii oo idii “every morning” 2 Kgs 16:15, icɔl oo icɔl “every evening” 1 Kgs 17:6, ipaangu oo ipaangu “every month” 1 Kgs 5:28, lepola oo lepola “each tribe” Deut 1:23.

    The same construction (the “distributive”) is described for Southern Kissi: a noun repeated around a particle ó, with the class pronoun prefixed outside the o class, and a prefix ì- before certain time nouns (Childs 1995, p. 197-198).

    “Someone, something”: reduplication¶

    Reduplication with a hyphen forms the indefinites for persons and things:

    • vana-vana “someone, anyone”; with negation, “nobody”: vana-vana ve lasiɛmbu “nobody was ashamed” Gen 2:25;
    • ɲɛ-ɲɛ “something, anything”; with negation, “nothing”: ɲɛ-ɲɛ ve lecoo le “there was nothing on it” Gen 1:2.

    “The same, a single”: agreement¶

    pilɛ “one” forms an indefinite that agrees with the class of the noun, in the sense of “the same, a single”:

    Class Form Example
    o pilɔɔ baa pilɔɔ “in one place” Gen 1:9
    i pilɛi dialii pilɛi “one flesh” Gen 2:24
    len pilɛlen Paale pilɛlen “the same day” Gen 17:26
    lan pilɛlan diomnda pilɛlan “the same words” Gen 11:1

    In the 1979 description, this series is written pilöö, pilèi, pilèlen, pilèlan (Keita 1979, p. 71).

    Spelling rules

    • Indefinites are written separately from the noun: po celen, yowva kpede.
    • Reduplicated indefinites take a hyphen: vana-vana, ɲɛ-ɲɛ.
    • X oo X is written as three words: visi oo visi.

    1979 usage

    The 1979 description also cites kèkè “any” and yööli “all” (Keita 1979, p. 71), which do not appear in the Bible; the Bible uses X oo X and kpow. These words remain to be checked with speakers. Southern Kissi has kɛ́kɛ̀ “any”, preceded by the class pronoun (ò-kɛ́kɛ̀ “anyone, anything”) (Childs 1995, p. 160).
