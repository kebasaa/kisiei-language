    Interrogatives¶

    The rules on this page are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible (BKG), compared with the 1979 description (Keita 1979, p. 64-69).

    Question words¶

    Word Meaning Example (Bible)
    nɛ̀ɛ́ who? (subject) Nɛ̀ɛ́ cɔm yɛ nɔm “Who showed you…?” Gen 3:11
    nɛ̀ɛ́-nɛ̀ who (is it)? Nɔm nɛ̀ɛ́-nɛ̀ “Who are you?” Gen 27:18
    yɛɛ what? which? Dɛnɛ yɛɛ n tosa yɛ hoo “What have you done?” Gen 3:13
    le yɛɛ why? Le yɛɛ n tambu yɛ kɔl “Why are you angry?” Gen 4:6
    kuɛɛ where?; which? Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ “Where are you?” Gen 3:9; Langbanɔ kuɛɛ co yɛ hoo “Who is this man?” Gen 24:65
    vɛ̀ɛ̀ is it that…? how? Vɛ̀ɛ̀ o co kɛndɛ lon “Is he well?” Gen 29:6
    bàà is it really…? bàà mà tɛɛmbuu nda “will you really destroy them?” Gen 18:24

    The same words exist in Southern Kissi: kùɛ́ɛ́ “where”, nɛ̀ɛ́ “who”, yɛ̀ɛ́ “what”, wɛ̀ɛ́ “how, how much” (Childs 1995, p. 111, 264); the initial w there corresponds to the v of the Bible (vɛ̀ɛ̀).

    Building the question¶

    Content questions (who, what, where…)¶

    1. The question word is placed at the start of the sentence, or just after the noun it determines (Dɛnɛ yɛɛ “what thing”, Langbanɔ kuɛɛ “which man”).
    2. It is often followed by the particle nɛ, which is optional and makes no notable difference in meaning (likewise in Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 144)): Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ “Where are you?”, Yɛɛ nɛ kendu yɛ a Mɛlɛka “Is anything too hard for the Lord?” Gen 18:14. In Southern Kissi, this particle (-nɛ́) is optional and does not change the meaning (Childs 1995, p. 111, 265).
    3. The verb is followed by the particle yɛ, which closes the question: n co yɛ, n tosa yɛ. In the future, yɛ attaches to -nin: yɛnin (vɛ̀ɛ̀ ya sina yɛnin “how shall I know?” Gen 15:8).

    The subject pronoun takes the form of the relative series: n “you (sing.)”, ya “I”, naa n “we”, niaa “you (plur.)” (Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ “Where are you?”; kuɛɛ niaa fula yɛ “where do you come from?” Gen 29:4). See Relative clauses.

    The question-final particle is already described in 1979 (yè, “a particle that appears after the verb”) (Keita 1979, p. 67). Southern Kissi has the same particle yɛ̀, placed after the conjugated verb; it is absent when the clause has no conjugated verb (Childs 1995, p. 111, 265).

    Yes/no questions¶

    A yes/no question opens with vɛ̀ɛ̀: Vɛ̀ɛ̀ a yeema mì la kɔɛ niaa langbanɔɔ ho “Will you go with this man?” Gen 24:58. Without a question word, only the rising voice marks the question; in writing, the question mark is enough (Keita 1979, p. 69). In Southern Kissi, this is the general case: a yes/no question differs from a statement only by its rising intonation (Childs 1995, p. 264).

    bàà introduces a question that expects a negative answer or expresses surprise Gen 20:4.

    Spelling rules

    • Question words are written separately: le yɛɛ, Dɛnɛ yɛɛ.
    • nɛ̀ɛ́ and vɛ̀ɛ̀ carry their tone marks, as in the Bible; this distinguishes them from nɛɛ and vɛɛ.
    • The question ends with ? with no space before it; inside quotation marks: «Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ?».

    1979 usage

    The 1979 description gives three interrogative adjectives (köè “which”, ngö “which” before the noun, nèènè “whose”) and three pronouns (nèè “who”, kuè “which one”, ngö) (Keita 1979, p. 64-67). The Bible uses nɛ̀ɛ́ “who”, kuɛɛ “where, which” and yɛɛ “what”; the forms köè and ngö do not appear there and remain to be checked with speakers.
