    Noun classes¶

    The principle¶

    Every Kisiei noun consists of a stem, which carries the meaning, and a class suffix (also called morpheme or nominal modality (Keita 1979, p. 20-22)), which indicates the class of the noun and its number:

    Noun Stem Suffix Meaning
    yɔmndo yɔm -ndo tree
    cɛiyo cɛi -yo house
    diolan diola -n (lan class) name
    mɛnndan mɛn- -ndan water
    nawvo naw -vo cow

    The suffix is not just a word ending: it comes back on the words that accompany the noun (determiners, pronouns, relatives). This is the agreement system described on the Agreement page.

    Full form and stem form¶

    The noun has two forms:

    • the full form (stem + suffix): when the noun stands alone, or at the end of a phrase;
    • the stem form (stem alone): when the noun is followed by a word that determines it — adjective, demonstrative, possessive, number, noun complement. The suffix then moves onto that word.
    Full form Stem form + determiner
    cɛiyo “the house” cɛi kon “that house”
    yɔmndo “the tree” yɔm kon “that tree”
    diolan “the name” diola ndɔlan “his name”
    kalaa “the mother” kala ndɔ “his mother”
    pɔmbɔɔ “the younger sibling” pɔmbɔ ndɔ “his younger brother”

    The stem form is also called the indefinite form, and the full form the definite form (Keita 1979, p. 20); the full form indeed plays the role of the French definite article, which Kisiei does not have.

    Rule

    The suffix is never written twice: cɛiyo kon is wrong; write cɛi kon.

    lan, len and an classes: stem + class pronoun¶

    In the o class, the stem form is the bare stem (cɛi kon). In the lan, len and an classes, the Bible (BKG) writes before the determiner the stem followed by the class pronoun without its final nasal: -la, -le, -ma. After a nasal, -le becomes -nde; after l, -lle. The 1979 manual mentions this only for the an class (Keita 1979, p. 122); the same construction is described in Southern Kissi (“noun + class pronoun + adjective”) (Childs 1995, p. 150).

    Full form Stem form Example (Bible)
    diolan “name” diola diola ndalan “their names” Gen 10:32
    kobilan “cities” kobila kobila lan “these cities” Gen 19:25
    lɛŋnden “land” lɛŋnde lɛŋnde Havila len “the land of Havilah” Gen 2:11
    bollen “head” bolle bolle ndɔlen “its top” Gen 11:4
    nilen “ear” nile nile tiilannde “the lobe of the ear” Exod 29:20
    mɛnndan “water” mɛmma mɛmma co lebɛngu-an “the waters that are below” Gen 1:7
    koovan “blood” kooma kooma pɔmbɔ nɔmndan “your brother’s blood” Gen 4:10
    cuɛiyan “oil” cuɛima

    Plurals in -lan follow the same rule: their stem form ends in -la (-nda after a nasal), as in the Bible: tɔŋnda nilan “my commandments” Gen 26:5, yungula “the families” Gen 10:32, vɔsila bɔɔ-bɔɔ “many years” Gen 24:1.

    Rule

    Before a determiner, write as a single word: stem + la (lan class), stem + le / nde / lle (len class), stem + ma (an class): diola ndɔlan, lɛŋnde len, mɛmma man.

    The classes¶

    Eight suffixes are distinguished (Keita 1979, p. 22-25), grouped here into classes according to their plural and their referents. The suffixes have variants that depend on the end of the stem (after a nasal, -nd- is found; after l, -l- or -t-; after w, -v-; after i, -y-). The same variants are described for Southern Kissi (Childs 1995, p. 151-156).

    Class Singular suffixes Typical content Examples (Bible)
    o / a / ɔ -o, -ndo, -vo, -lo, -yo; -a; -ɔ living beings (people, animals) and many objects yɔmndo “tree”, nawvo “cow”, kalaa “mother”, pɔmbɔɔ “younger sibling”, miallo “stranger”
    i -i, -ei, -ɛi certain solid objects, names of languages pelei “foot”, mɔɛi “knife”, kelɛi “bracelet”, sɔɛi “word”
    e -e, -ye, -nde, -ve, -le grains, countable objects, collections ciŋnde “tooth”, yɛiye “seed”
    an -an, -yan, -van, -ndan liquids, masses mɛnndan “water”, koovan “blood”, cuɛiyan “oil”
    lan -lan, -tan, -ndan abstract nouns; and the plural of many nouns diolan “name”, yolan “dance”
    len -len, -ten, -nden fruits, certain body parts, soft objects; diminutives nilen “ear”, bollen “head”, lɛŋnden “land”

    The i and e series form a single class in Southern Kissi: there the ending -e becomes -ei or -ɛi after a stem ending in a vowel (Childs 1995, p. 148, 153). The Bible points the same way: nouns of both series are taken up by the same pronoun i, have the same referent -e (see Agreement) and the same plural in -on.

    The main meanings are also found in Southern Kissi: grains and names of languages in the i class, diminutives in the le (len) class, liquids in the ma (an) class (Childs 1995, p. 165-169). Abstract nouns, on the other hand, belong there mostly to the le class (Childs 1995, p. 169).

    The plural is covered on the Plural page.

    Comparison: Southern Kissi

    For Southern Kissi, the classes are named after their pronoun (Childs 1995, p. 148-149): o, a, i, ŋ, le, la, ma. The most frequent singular/plural pairs are o/la (things), o/a (people, animals) and i/ŋ; next come le/i, le/la, le/ma and o/i. The plural in -on of the 1979 description (pelon, sɔɔn) corresponds to the ŋ class of that analysis. The same system is found in Kisiei of Guinea, with different forms in the details.

    What the Bible shows¶

    The Bible confirms the broad outlines of the system described in 1979: full form and stem form, class suffixes and repetition of the class on determiners. It adds two clarifications:

    1. Suffixes in -nd- keep the final nasal of the stem, whether it is n or ŋ: mɛnndan (stem mɛn-), but lɛŋnden (stem lɛŋ). See Consonants.
    2. When a noun phrase ends with a relative clause or a complement, the class of the noun is taken up again at the end of the phrase by a referent attached with a hyphen (kɔngɔ vesɛi fula-o). See Agreement.

    Masculine and feminine¶

    Kisiei has no grammatical gender. To specify the sex of a being, piaando “male” or laando “female” is added to the stem form (Keita 1979, p. 28): so piaando “stallion”, so laando “mare”. The same device is described in Southern Kissi for animals (Childs 1995, p. 216-217). The Bible uses these words in the plural for children: a piaandua a à laandua “sons and daughters” Gen 5:4.
