# Proper names and loanwords

## Kissi proper names

Kissi first names and surnames are written with the Kisiei alphabet like other words. The names given according to birth order, very frequent in the examples of the 1979 manual [[(Keita1979)]] — *Tamba, Saa, Fallo, Faya, Kumba, Sia, Finda, Teeva…* — contain no special letter and do not change.

For a name that contained *ty, nh, ny, è, ö* in the old spelling, apply the same correspondences as for other words (see [From the old to the new spelling](old-and-new.md)). In official documents (civil registers, identity cards), the name may keep the French spelling already in use; it is then **quoted** as it is, without being respelled.

## Foreign proper names

The Bible [[(BKG)]] adapts biblical names to the Kisiei alphabet, but keeps letters that do not exist in ordinary Kisiei words, in particular **r**:

| Name (French) | In Kisiei (Bible) |
| --- | --- |
| Abraham | {{Abraham}} |
| Israël | {{Israɛl}} |
| Égypte | {{Esipte}} |
| Jérusalem | {{Yerusalɛm}} |
| Benjamin | {{Bɛnyamɛn}} |

Note that:

- French *è* becomes **ɛ** ({{Israɛl}});
- *j* becomes **y** ({{Yerusalɛm}}, {{Bɛnyamɛn}});
- *z* and soft *g* are replaced by Kisiei sounds ({{Esipte}} for *Égypte*).

Names of peoples take Kisiei class suffixes: {{Israɛlla}} “the Israelites”, {{Aarɔn-nda}} “those of Aaron”.

## Loanwords

Kisiei has borrowed words from Maninka, French, English and Krio. Once integrated, a loanword is written with the Kisiei alphabet and takes a class suffix like a Kisiei word:

{{sɛbɛ}} “book, letter” (from Maninka), {{volion}} “money”, {{rɛsɛn}} “grape” (from French), {{kafo}} “festival, meeting”.

In Southern Kissi, a loanword adapts to the Kissi syllable: a consonant cluster is broken up by a vowel, and a word that would begin with a vowel often receives an *h*; {{Hala}} “God” thus comes from Arabic *Allah* [[(Childs1995, p. 64-65)]]. Loanwords there mostly enter class *o* in the singular and *la* in the plural [[(Childs1995, p. 163)]]. Biblical proper names, by contrast, keep their consonant clusters ({{Israɛl}}, {{Esipte}}).

!!! rule "Rule"
    - An **integrated** loanword is written in Kisiei spelling: do not use *é*, *è*, *ou* or *ch* in it ({{rɛsɛn}}, not *raisin*).
    - *r* may be kept in a loanword where it is pronounced ({{rɛsɛn}}).
    - A **non-integrated** foreign word (brand name, quotation) is put in italics and keeps its original spelling.

The varieties differ precisely in their loanwords: Kisiduei borrows from Maninka, Kpekeduei from Loma, Mende and Krio [[(Keita1979, p. 8-10)]] (see [Regional varieties](../introduction/dialects.md)). In a text intended for everyone, prefer the common Kisiei word when there is one.
