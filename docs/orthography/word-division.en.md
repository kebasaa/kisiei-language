# Word division and the hyphen

Knowing where a word begins and ends is one of the main difficulties in writing Kisiei. This chapter brings together the rules followed by the Bible [[(BKG)]].

## Full form and stem form of the noun

A noun has two written forms (see [Noun classes](../grammar/nouns/noun-classes.md)):

- the **full form**, with its class suffix, when the noun stands alone or at the end of a phrase: {{cɛiyo}} “the house”, {{yɔmndo}} “the tree”, {{diolan}} “the name”;
- the **stem form**, without a suffix, when the noun is followed by a determiner, an adjective, a number or a possessor: {{cɛi kon}} “that house”, {{yɔm kon}} “that tree”, {{diola ndɔlan}} “his/her name”.

!!! rule "Rule"
    The stem form is **a separate word**, separated by a space from what follows. Do not write *cɛikon* or *cɛi-kon*.

Southern Kissi has the same alternation: the noun's suffix moves onto the word that modifies it [[(Childs1995, p. 150-151)]].

## Grammatical words are written separately

Pronouns, auxiliaries, prepositions, demonstratives and possessives are written as separate words:

{{O co kɛndia le}} “he (she) is not well” [1 Sam 19:14](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/1SA.19.14.BKG) — *o* “he, she”, *co* “be”, *kɛndia* “in good health”, *le* (negation).

{{Mí Hala ca maa ɲɛ-tengaa naŋ}} “And God saw that the light was good” [Gen 1:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.4.BKG).

The final negation {{le}} / {{te}} is also a separate word; write {{te}} after *n*, *m*, *ŋ* or *l*, and {{le}} after a vowel or *w* (see [Negation](../grammar/verbs/negation.md); in Southern Kissi, *te* follows any consonant [[(Childs1995, p. 89, 125)]]): {{La kei nanu le}} “do not pass through here” [Num 20:20](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/NUM.20.20.BKG), {{Vana diinin nɔm te}} “no one will kill you” [2 Sam 19:24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/2SA.19.24.BKG).

## The hyphen

The Bible uses the hyphen a great deal (nearly 18,000 times). It has three uses.

### 1. The referent that closes a phrase

When a **noun phrase** ends with something other than the noun itself — a relative clause, a complement, a proper name — the noun's class is repeated by a **referent** (a class marker) which is **attached by a hyphen to the last word** of the phrase:

| Group | Referent | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| class *o* (singular) | *-o, -ndo, -yo, -lo* | {{kɔngɔ vesɛi fula-o}} “the dry land that appeared” [Gen 1:10](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.10.BKG) |
| | | {{Kɔl Hala-o}} “the Spirit of God” [Gen 1:2](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.2.BKG) |
| | | {{o co paalen-ndo}} “the one that is the sun” [Gen 1:16](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.16.BKG) |
| class *a* (plural, persons) | *-a, -nda* | {{visia pɔmbɔa kela lɛŋ-nda}} “the small animals that walk on the ground” [Gen 1:24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.24.BKG) |
| class *an* | *-an* | {{mɛmma co lebɛngu-an}} “the waters that are below” [Gen 1:7](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.7.BKG) |
| class *e / i* | *-e, -nde* | {{sɔɛi Mɛlɛka dimul ndu-e}} “the word that the Lord had spoken to him” [Gen 15:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.15.6.BKG) |

The choice between *-o*, *-ndo*, *-yo* or *-lo* (and likewise *-a / -nda*, *-e / -nde*) depends on the end of the word to which the referent is attached: after a nasal write *-ndo*, *-nda*, *-nde* ({{lɛŋnden-ndo}}, {{nin-nda}}); elsewhere the simple form ({{fula-o}}, {{coo-an}}). The [Agreement page](../grammar/agreement.md) gives these forms in detail.

!!! keita "1979 usage"
    In 1979, these referents are presented as separate **relative pronouns** (*vo, ve, van, lan…*), written after the verb [[(Keita1979, p. 88-89)]]: *Domaa n pendu vo sèsa* “the shirt that you bought has torn”. The Bible writes the same marker **attached by a hyphen** at the end of the relative clause; the form *vo* almost never appears on its own. Follow the Bible. The description of Southern Kissi points the same way: the noun's class ending is a suffix placed at the end of the relative clause [[(Childs1995, p. 285-286)]].

### 2. Compound words

The hyphen joins the elements of a **compound noun** whose parts keep their form (in Southern Kissi, these non-fixed compounds are pronounced as separate words [[(Childs1995, p. 216)]]): {{Cɛi-Hala-o}} “the house of God” [Gen 28:17](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.28.17.BKG), {{fuɲa-mamaa}} “ancestor”, {{fonda-loamaa-lasálàá}} “altar (place where sacrifices are burnt)”, {{cɛi-kola}} “tent (house of cloth)”.

Very common compounds are often written as a single word: {{vanapɔnɔɔ}} “man”, {{vanalanɔɔ}} “woman”, {{halataalaa}} “sky”. See [Compound nouns](../grammar/nouns/compounds.md).

### 3. Reduplicated words

A word repeated to express intensity, distribution or repetition is written with a hyphen: {{taw-taw}} “very much indeed”, {{ɲɛ-ɲɛ}} “anything at all”, {{yɛ́-yɛ́}} “each kind”, {{bɛndu-bɛnduan}} “very big (ones)”.

## The prefix n'

The nasal prefix *n'* is attached by an apostrophe to the following word: {{n'van tɔ́}} “ten people”. See [Consonants](consonants.md).

## Summary

| Case | Write | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| noun alone | full form, one word | {{cɛiyo}} |
| noun + determiner | stem form + space | {{cɛi kon}} |
| phrase closed by a referent | hyphen | {{fula-o}} |
| compound | hyphen (or a single word if lexicalised) | {{fuɲa-mamaa}} |
| reduplication | hyphen | {{taw-taw}} |
| grammatical words | separate words | {{O co kɛndia le}} |
