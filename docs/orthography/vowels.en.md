# Vowels

## The seven vowels

Kisiei has seven vowels, like Southern Kissi [[(Childs1995, p. 35)]]. Two of them are written with letters that are not part of the English alphabet.

| Letter | Pronunciation | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **i** | *i* of *machine* | {{pilɛ}} | one |
| **e** | close *e*, like *ay* in *say* but without the glide towards *ee* | {{pelei}} | foot |
| **ɛ** | open *e*, as in *bed* | {{lɛŋ}} | ground, earth |
| **a** | *a* of *father* | {{bala}} | hands |
| **ɔ** | open *o*, as in *law* (British *thought*) | {{pɔmbɔɔ}} | small, younger |
| **o** | close *o*, like *o* in *go* but without the glide towards *oo* | {{yondoo}} | bush |
| **u** | *oo* of *food* | {{cuaa}} | child |

English speakers should keep every vowel **pure**: Kisiei *e* and *o* do not glide like English *say* and *go*.

!!! rule "Rule"
    Always distinguish **e / ɛ** and **o / ɔ**: they are different vowels, which change the meaning of words. Do not write *é*, *è* or *ö*: the *è* and *ö* of the old spelling become **ɛ** and **ɔ** (see [From the old to the new spelling](old-and-new.md)).

## Long vowels

A **long** vowel is pronounced for longer than a short vowel; it is written by **doubling** the letter: *aa, ee, ɛɛ, ii, oo, ɔɔ, uu*. Length changes the meaning of a word [[(Childs1995, p. 37-38)]]; it is the most frequent source of errors among learners [[(Keita1979, p. 12)]].

| Short | Meaning | Long | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| {{velu}} | beget | {{veelu}} | call |
| {{yema}} | sister | {{yeema}} | want |
| {{bala}} | hands | {{baala}} | work |
| {{koma}} | then, again | {{kooma}} | blood |

Examples from the Bible: {{Mɛɛ Adam velu Sɛt}} “when Adam begat Seth” [Gen 5:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.5.4.BKG); {{Mí Mɛlɛka Hala veelu vanapɔnɔɔ}} “The Lord God called the man” [Gen 3:9](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.3.9.BKG).

### Final long vowel and the full form

Very often, the long vowel at the end of a word is not part of the stem: it is the **class suffix** of a noun or the **infinitive** marker of a verb. It disappears when the word takes its stem form:

| Stem form | Full form |
| --- | --- |
| {{kala ndɔ}} “his/her mother” | {{kalaa}} “the mother” |
| {{fula}} “go out (he goes out)” | {{fulaa}} “to go out, the going out” |
| {{dio}} “eat (he eats)” | {{dioo}} “to eat, the eating” |

So do not “correct” {{kala ndɔ}} to *kalaa ndɔ*: the short form is the right one before a determiner (see [Word division](word-division.md) and [Noun classes](../grammar/nouns/noun-classes.md)).

## Vowel sequences

Two different vowels can follow each other; they are written as they are heard, without a diaeresis or apostrophe:

- *ia, io, iɔ, iɛ*: {{fuɲaa}}, {{dioo}}, {{tiɔn}}, {{ciɛɔn}};
- *ua, uɛ, ui*: {{cuaa}}, {{ŋuɛɛnu}}, {{kuí}};
- *ɛi, ei, oi*: {{cɛiyo}}, {{pelei}}, {{hoeiye}}.

These sequences are described in 1979 as diphthongs and triphthongs [[(Keita1979, p. 11)]]. In Southern Kissi, *i* and *u* before another vowel are pronounced as the semivowels [j] and [w], while sequences ending in *i* or *u* (*ei, ɛi, oi, au…*) have the length of a long vowel [[(Childs1995, p. 39-42)]]. The Bible [[(BKG)]] always writes them with the ordinary letters.

## Nasal vowels

When a vowel is followed by **n** at the end of a syllable, it is pronounced nasalised: *an, ɛn, in, on, ɔn, un*. No sign is added; simply write the vowel followed by *n*:

{{diolan}} “name”, {{mɛnndan}} “water”, {{nin}} “in”, {{kon}} “that…”, {{sɔɔn}} “words”, {{hun}} “come”.

In Southern Kissi, this final nasal is analysed as a consonant [ŋ] that nasalises the preceding vowel; it would continue earlier nasal vowels [[(Childs1995, p. 88-89)]].

!!! note "Note"
    At the end of a stem, the nasal sound can be [n] or [ŋ], and the Bible distinguishes them: {{nan}} “pull” ({{má nan Yosɛf}} “they pulled Joseph” [Gen 37:28](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.37.28.BKG)) is not {{naŋ}} “good”. See [Consonants](consonants.md#n-and).

The sequences *am, om, im…* are not nasal vowels, but a vowel followed by the consonant *m* [[(Keita1979, p. 13)]]: {{yɔmndo}} is divided *yɔm-ndo*.
