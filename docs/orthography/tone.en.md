# Tone

## A tone language

Kisiei is a **tone language**: the pitch of the voice on a syllable can change the meaning of a word or the grammatical value of a form. Two levels are distinguished, **high** and **low**, which can combine on a long syllable into **high-falling** and **low-rising** [[(Keita1979, p. 17-18)]]; Southern Kissi has the same inventory [[(Childs1995, p. 43)]]. Here are some minimal pairs (respelled forms):

| Tone | Word | Meaning | | Tone | Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| high-fall. | {{kɔɔ\|r}} | kind of snake | ≠ | low-rise. | {{kɔɔ\|r}} | back |
| low-rise. | {{baa\|r}} | hand | ≠ | high-fall. | {{baa\|r}} | price |
| low-rise. | {{bɔlɔɔ\|r}} | banana | ≠ | high-fall. | {{bɔlɔɔ\|r}} | bag |

Tone is also used in conjugation: *i kela* “I walk about (usually)” and *i kela* “I walked about” differ only in tone [[(Keita1979, p. 18)]] (see [Tense, aspect and mood](../grammar/verbs/tense-aspect-mood.md)).

## How the Bible marks tones

The old spelling marked no tones. The Bible [[(BKG)]] marks tone with accents:

- the **acute accent** ( ´ ) for **high tone**: {{mí}}, {{náŋ}};
- the **grave accent** ( ` ) for **low tone**: {{mì}}, {{nàà}};
- on a long vowel, one accent per vowel: {{ndóò}} (high-low), {{màá}} (low-high), {{háá}} (high-high).

But it does **not** mark them everywhere: about **one word in ten** carries an accent. The accents are concentrated on two kinds of words.

### 1. Grammatical words whose tone changes their function

These are mainly the small words that link clauses. Their tone is always marked:

| High tone | Use | Low tone | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| {{mí}} | “and (then)”, past narrative: {{Mí Hala dimi aa}} “And God said” [Gen 1:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.3.BKG) | {{mì}} | “and (that)”, intended or future sequence: {{mì kɔngɔ vesɛiyo lo}} “and let the dry land appear” [Gen 1:9](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.9.BKG) |
| {{mó}} | the same, + *o* “he, she”: {{mó sɛi}} “and he separated” [Gen 1:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.4.BKG) | {{mò}} | the same, + *o*: {{mò vel naa}} “that he may resemble us” [Gen 1:26](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.26.BKG) |
| {{má}} | the same, + *a* “they”: {{má kɔa yondoo nin}} “and they went into the bush” [Gen 4:8](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.4.8.BKG) | {{mà}} | the same, + *a*: {{mà va kelaa}} “that they may fly” [Gen 1:20](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.20.BKG) |

Other small words always carry their tone: {{háá}} “until” (to be distinguished from {{haa}} “these”), {{ndóò}} (past auxiliary, see [Tense](../grammar/verbs/tense-aspect-mood.md)), {{ndáà}} “them” (beside {{ndaa}} “their”), {{tùú}} in {{le tùú}} “because”, {{tɔ̀a}} “all”.

### 2. Words that would be confused without an accent

Some words carry an accent in almost all their occurrences, because they have a homograph: {{sálà}} “offering”, {{lasálàá}} “sacrifice”, {{siòònde}} “fear”, {{dɔ́ɔ́ngɔ́}} “tent”, {{hɔ́ɔ́yɛi}} “freedom”.

### Other words

Common nouns, verbs and adjectives carry **no** accent: {{pɔmbɔɔ}}, {{cɛiyo}}, {{tosa}}, {{naŋ}}. When the same word appears in the Bible both with and without an accent, the form without an accent is generally by far the more frequent ({{naŋ}} rather than {{náŋ}}). In Southern Kissi, most nouns have only low tones, and the tone of verbs is set by the conjugation rather than by the word itself [[(Childs1995, p. 54-55)]].

!!! rule "Practical rule"
    1. **Always** mark the tone on {{mí}}/{{mì}}, {{mó}}/{{mò}}, {{má}}/{{mà}} and on the small words in the list above.
    2. Mark the tone on a content word **only if it could be confused** with another word in the context, and then follow the Bible's form.
    3. In all other cases, **do not write** an accent.
    4. One accent per vowel: a long vowel gets two ({{ndóò}}, {{háá}}).

!!! note "Note"
    The exact tone rules of Kisiei (basic tones of words, tone changes in the sentence) are described neither in the 1979 manual nor in the Bible. They are among the [open questions](../reference/open-questions.md). For Southern Kissi they are described in detail [[(Childs1995, p. 43-65, 91-98)]], but it is not yet known which of them also hold for Kisiei.

## Intonation

The sentence also has an **intonation** [[(Keita1979, p. 17)]]: a question ends with a rise in the voice, a statement with a fall. In writing, this is shown simply by the question mark or the full stop: {{A hin?}} “Are you coming?”, {{I hin.}} “I am coming.”

In Southern Kissi, only the closed question (answer yes or no) ends with a rise; a question with a question word ends, like a statement, with a fall [[(Childs1995, p. 60-61, 264)]].
