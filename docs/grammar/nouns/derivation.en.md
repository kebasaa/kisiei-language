# Derivation

Derivation forms new nouns by adding a **suffix** or a **prefix** to a stem. A distinction is made between *pure derivatives* (suffixes) and *lexical derivatives* (former nouns that have become prefixes) [[(Keita1979, p. 34-40)]]. Unless otherwise stated, the examples below are taken from the Bible [[(BKG)]].

## Suffixes

### -nɔɔ: person who does, person of

The suffix **-nɔɔ** (plural **-nda** or **-a**) forms nouns for people:

- **origin, people**: {{Esiptenɔɔ}} “Egyptian” [Gen 16:1](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.16.1.BKG), {{Israɛlnɔɔ}} “Israelite”, {{Filistinɔɔ}} “Philistine”;
- **occupation, function**: {{kpalilnɔɔ}} “guard, watchman” [2 Sam 18:24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/2SA.18.24.BKG), {{kiilanɔɔ}} “messenger” [Gen 50:16](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.50.16.BKG), {{mandenɔɔ}} “marabout, diviner”;
- **quality**: {{kuubanɔɔ}} “hero”, {{langbanɔɔ}} “grown man”.

Southern Kissi has the same agent suffix, *-nɔ̀ɔ́*, with a plural in *-a* [[(Childs1995, p. 198-199)]].

The same suffix also forms family names (*Kambadunɔɔ*) and names for speakers of a language ({{Kisinɔɔ|r}} “a Kisi person”) [[(Keita1979, p. 35)]].

### -yɛi: quality, state

The suffix **-yɛi** forms abstract nouns:

| Base | Derivative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {{vana}} “person” | {{vanayɛi}} | kinship |
| {{cuaambɔɔ}} “child” | {{cuaambɔyɛi}} | childhood |
| *felengoo* “young” | {{felengiyɛi}} | youth [Gen 8:21](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.8.21.BKG) |
| *vanakɛndɛ* “good person” | {{vanakɛndɛyɛi}} | goodness, righteousness [1 Sam 24:19](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/1SA.24.19.BKG) |
| *hɔ́ɔ́* “free” | {{hɔ́ɔ́yɛi}} | freedom [Exod 21:2](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/EXO.21.2.BKG) |

### -ndɔɔ: rank (ordinals)

Added to a number, **-ndɔɔ** forms the ordinal: {{ŋɔmmɛwndɔɔ}} “seventh”, like *-ndɔ̀ɔ́* in Southern Kissi [[(Childs1995, p. 200-201)]]. See [Numbers](../determiners/numerals.md).

### -len: diminutive

The suffix **-len** (*len* class) can form **diminutives** [[(Keita1979, p. 23, 35)]]: {{pɔmbɔlen}} “very small”, and, from animal names, nouns for human faults or qualities (*tunnden* “filth”, from {{tunndo}} “dog”). In Southern Kissi too, the *le* class is used to form diminutives and most abstract nouns [[(Childs1995, p. 166, 203)]].

### i-…-ei: state

A discontinuous form *i-…-ei* forms nouns of state [[(Keita1979, p. 37)]]: *ilafilafiei* “always being in a hurry”. This type is rare in the Bible; it remains to be confirmed with speakers.

### le-: number of times

Prefixed to a number, **le-** expresses repetition [[(Keita1979, p. 35)]]: {{leyaa}} “three times”, {{leŋɔmmɛw}} “seven times”.

## Prefixes derived from nouns

Several nouns are used as the first element of many derivatives; they then lose their suffix:

| Prefix | Source noun | Derivatives (Bible) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ɲɛ-** “thing” | {{ɲɛ}} | {{ɲɛdiaa}} “food”, {{ɲɛkɛndɛi}} “good thing, happiness”, {{ɲɛ-sɔlaa}} “wealth”, {{ɲɛ-tengaa}} “light” |
| **kɔl-** “heart” | {{kɔllo}} | {{kɔlɲuloo}} “comfort, rest” [Gen 5:29](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.5.29.BKG), {{kɔlvesɛiyo}} “violence” [Gen 6:13](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.6.13.BKG), {{kɔltuwvo}} “anger” [Exod 11:8](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/EXO.11.8.BKG), {{kɔlkɛndɔɔ}} “joy” |
| **vana-** “person” | {{vana}} | {{vanacieo}} “human being”, {{vanayɛi}} “kinship”, {{vana-solia-lasálàá}} “priest” |
| **dɛnɛ** “deed, thing” | {{dɛnɛ}} | {{dɛnɛ kɛndɔɔ}} “good”, {{dɛnɛ vɔɔndo}} “evil” [Gen 2:9](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.9.BKG) |

**vana-** corresponds to the agent prefix *wànà-* of Southern Kissi, which is likewise derived from the word “person” [[(Childs1995, p. 199-200)]]. The word “thing” there likewise enters into so many compounds that it becomes almost a prefix [[(Childs1995, p. 216)]].

!!! keita "1979 usage"
    In 1979 one also finds *ba-* “hand” (*bamɛllo* “left hand”) and *dɛnɛ-* written attached (*dɛnɛkɛndɔɔ*) [[(Keita1979, p. 38-39)]]. The Bible writes {{dɛnɛ}} as a separate word: {{dɛnɛ kɛndɔɔ}}.

## Reduplication

Reduplicating a word or a stem forms a derivative expressing intensity, distribution or repetition; it is written with a hyphen: {{taw-taw}} “really a lot”, {{ɲɛ-ɲɛ}} “anything at all”, {{vana-vana}} “anyone at all”, {{bɛndu-bɛnduan}} “very great”.
