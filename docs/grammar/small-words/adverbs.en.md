# Adverbs and ideophones

Adverbs are invariable; they are usually placed **after the verb and its objects**, at the end of the sentence or just before the negation [[(Keita1979, p. 116)]]. The forms and examples below are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible [[(BKG)]].

## Ideophones

Ideophones are expressive words that depict a sound, a manner, an intensity or a visual impression; they follow the verb or adjective they reinforce and are often reduplicated. They form a class of their own, with a particular prosody, described in detail for Southern Kissi [[(Childs1995, p. 132-140)]]. Examples from the Bible:

- {{idiiyo púŋ}} “early in the morning, at dawn” [Gen 21:14](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.21.14.BKG);
- {{mà faŋan … few-few}} “go away at once!” [Gen 12:19](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.12.19.BKG);
- {{hoeyii ken niinu … kpɛŋ}} “these locusts covered (the land) entirely” [Exod 10:13](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/EXO.10.13.BKG);
- {{o puiyaa tɛi-tɛi}} “well watered everywhere” [Gen 13:10](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.13.10.BKG).

!!! rule "Spelling rules"
    - Adverbs are written as separate words: {{kandɔɔ hau}}, {{n naŋ taw}}.
    - A **reduplicated** ideophone is written with a hyphen: {{few-few}}, {{tɛi-tɛi}}; the Bible often marks the tone on it ({{púŋ}}).
    - Fixed expressions are written as separate words: {{le fɛɛn a fɛɛn}}.

!!! keita "1979 usage"
    The 1979 description gives lists of adverbs of place, time, manner and quantity in the old orthography (*kpèèlun, levil, nanu, lon, lebèngu*; *lapöm, haw, siödii, diilèn, sunsun*; *taw, faw, tyapo*…) [[(Keita1979, p. 116)]]. Most of them are found in the Bible in their current form ({{nanu}}, {{lon}}, {{hau}}, {{sunsun}}, {{diilɛn}}, {{taw}}, {{faw}}).
