# Derived verbs

From a basic verb, Kisiei forms new verbs by **suffixation** or by **changing the stem**. The 1979 description distinguishes four kinds: reflexive verbs, reciprocal verbs, verbs “of help” (for someone's benefit) and verbs of repetition [[(Keita1979, p. 92-94)]]. The description of Southern Kissi speaks of **verbal extensions** and also counts four: causative, benefactive, middle and plural [[(Childs1995, p. 171-194)]]. The two classifications largely overlap; the forms and examples below are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible [[(BKG)]].

| Derivation | Form | Base → derived (Bible) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benefactive (“for, to someone”) | *-l*, *-ul* | {{dimi}} “say” → {{dimul}} “say to”; {{tosa}} “do” → {{tosal}} “do for”; {{soli}} “offer” → {{solul}} “offer to”; {{sɛva}} “write” → {{sɛval}} “write to” |
| Middle, reflexive, passive | *-n*, *-nun*, *-nndo* | {{cɔm}} “show” → {{cɔmnun}} “show oneself, appear”; {{tosa}} → {{tosan}} “be done, happen”; {{pulun}} / {{pulɔnndo}} “wash oneself” |
| Reciprocal | *-an* | {{yil}} “greet” → {{yilan}} “greet each other” |
| Causative | *-i-* | {{hiow}} “pass” → {{hivia}} “make pass”; {{vesa}} “be dry” → {{veesiaa}} “dry up (something)” |
| Plural, intensive | long vowel + *-uu* | {{kumbu}} “cover” → {{kuumbuu}} “cover over (completely)”; {{tɛɛmbuu}} “destroy” |

## Benefactive: *-l*, *-ul*

The benefactive shows that the action is done **for** or **addressed to** someone, who becomes the direct object:

- {{mó dimul nda aa}} “and he said to them” [Gen 1:28](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.28.BKG) (from {{dimi}} “say”);
- {{ɲɛ Ham po ndɔ tosal ndu-o}} “what his son Ham had done to him” [Gen 9:24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.9.24.BKG);
- {{mó solul Mɛlɛka sálàá}} “and he offered a sacrifice to the Lord” [Gen 4:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.4.3.BKG).

The long form doubles the *l*: {{sɛvallo}} “write for someone” [Judg 8:14](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/JDG.8.14.BKG), as the 1979 description already notes (*sèvallo*, *mandallo*) [[(Keita1979, p. 93)]]. The Southern Kissi benefactive has the same form *-(l)ul* [[(Childs1995, p. 178-184)]].

## Middle: *-n*, *-nun*

The middle turns the action back towards the subject: a **reflexive** meaning (“oneself”), a **passive** one (“be done”) or an **intransitive** one:

- {{Mí Mɛlɛka cɔmnun Abram}} “The Lord appeared to Abram” [Gen 12:7](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.12.7.BKG) (from {{cɔm}} “show”);
- {{Mí ɲɔɔ tosan lende}} “And it was so” [Gen 1:7](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.7.BKG);
- {{yɔŋ vɔɔndo va tosanndo}} “evil was being done” [Gen 6:11](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.6.11.BKG);
- {{le pulɔnndo}} “to wash herself” [Exod 2:5](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/EXO.2.5.BKG).

The long form of the middle ends in *-nndo* ({{tosanndo}}, {{pulɔnndo}}, {{pilanndo}}). The 1979 description presents these verbs as **reflexive pronominal verbs** in *-ndo* and *-nɔnndo* (*susanndo* “rub oneself”, *diinɔnndo* “kill oneself”) [[(Keita1979, p. 92-93)]]; for Southern Kissi, the middle (*-nuŋ*) is the only extension that carries a tone of its own [[(Childs1995, p. 184-190)]].

## Reciprocal: *-an*

The reciprocal expresses a mutual action: {{Má yilan}} “They greeted each other” [2 Kgs 10:15](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/2KI.10.15.BKG). The 1979 description gives *-anndo* and *-lanndo* for the long form (*yilanndo* “greet each other”, *vaalanndo* “farm for each other”) [[(Keita1979, p. 93)]]; these long forms do not appear in the Bible.

## Causative: *-i-*

The causative adds or inserts an *i* and often changes the stem vowel; it means “cause to do, make”:

- {{hiow}} “pass” → {{hivia}} “make pass”: {{vana hivia isɔɔ}} “an interpreter (one who makes words pass across)” [Gen 42:23](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.42.23.BKG);
- {{vesa}} “be dry” → {{veesiaa}} “dry up (something)” [Gen 41:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.41.6.BKG).

The 1979 description classes these verbs among the **verbs of repetition** formed with the infix *i* (*veesaa* → *veesiaa* “make dry”, *hiovöö* → *hivioo* “make pass”) [[(Keita1979, p. 94)]]. For Southern Kissi, the causative is also *-i*, and some verbs have the same form in the causative and the plural [[(Childs1995, p. 175-178, 190)]].

## Plural: long vowel and *-uu*

The plural indicates a repeated action, one affecting several objects or one done by several subjects; it lengthens the stem vowel and ends the verb in *-uu* [[(Childs1995, p. 190-194)]]:

- {{kumbu}} “cover” ({{Yondoo fulá mò kumbu kɔngɔ vesɛiyo}} [Gen 1:11](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.11.BKG)) → {{kuumbuu}} ({{má kuumbuu doma bɛndu kon ndu coo}} “they spread the garment over him” [Gen 9:23](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.9.23.BKG));
- {{tɛɛmbuu}} “destroy, wipe out” [Gen 6:17](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.6.17.BKG).

## Reduplication

Reduplicating the verb expresses repetition or spreading of the action; it is written as one word or with a hyphen: {{misimisi}} “devour” [Gen 41:20](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.41.20.BKG). The 1979 description gives *kèsikesio* “arrange”, *kilikilio* “cut into small pieces” [[(Keita1979, p. 94)]].

!!! rule "Spelling rules"
    - Derivational suffixes are written **joined** to the verb: {{dimul}}, {{cɔmnun}}, {{yilan}}, {{kuumbuu}}.
    - The derived verb keeps the spelling of the base verb, except where a vowel change is actually pronounced ({{hiow}} → {{hivia}}).
