# Personal pronouns

Kisiei has two main series of personal pronouns: the **subject** pronouns, which are short and placed before the verb, and the **independent** pronouns, which serve as objects, as emphasized subjects and as possessives for persons [[(Keita1979, p. 85-87)]].

## Table

| Person | Subject | Independent / object | Emphatic | Example (Bible) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st sing. | {{i}} | {{ya}} | {{ya pila}} “myself” | {{I tuisinin nɔm}} “I will put on you” [Gen 3:16](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.3.16.BKG) |
| 2nd sing. | {{a}} | {{nɔm}} | {{nɔm pila}} | {{le nɔm pila}} “for yourself” [Gen 14:21](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.14.21.BKG) |
| 3rd sing. | {{o}} | {{ndu}} | {{ndu pila}} | {{mó yaa nda}} “he brought them” [Gen 2:19](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.19.BKG) |
| 1st plur. | {{n}} | {{naa}} | {{naa pila}} | {{N kɔ delnun ndu coo}} “let us go and throw ourselves on him” [Gen 37:20](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.37.20.BKG) |
| 2nd plur. | {{la}} | {{niaa}} | {{niaa pila}} | {{Niaa pila bɛɛ la sina}} “you yourselves know” [Gen 31:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.31.6.BKG) |
| 3rd plur. | {{a}} | {{nda}} | {{nda pila}} | {{A ve sina}} “they did not know” [Gen 42:23](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.42.23.BKG) |

!!! rule "Spelling rules"
    - Pronouns are written **separately** from the verb: {{I tuei}}, {{N kɔɛ}}.
    - The emphatic word {{pila}} “self” is written **separately**: {{ya pila}}, {{nda pila}} (1979: *yapila* [[(Keita1979, p. 85)]]).
    - The 2nd person singular is written **{{nɔm}}** (1979: *num*, corrected by hand to *nöm* [[(Keita1979, p. 85)]]).

In relative clauses and questions, subjects take another series: {{ya}}, {{n}} “you (sing.)”, {{o}}, {{naa n}}, {{niaa}}, {{ndáà}}; see [Relative clauses](relative.md#the-subject-of-the-relative-clause).

## 3rd-person pronouns by class

When the pronoun replaces a thing, it takes the form of the noun’s class (see [Agreement](../agreement.md)):

| Class | Subject | Object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *o* (sing.) | {{o}} | {{ndu}} |
| plural persons | {{a}} | {{nda}} |
| *i* | {{i}} | {{ndi}} |
| plur. in *-on* | {{n}} | {{mun}} |
| *an* | {{ma}} | {{man}} |
| *len* | {{le}} | {{len}} |
| *lan* | {{la}} | {{lan}} |

Examples: {{Tɛyii tasei diolan Pison, ndi balu lɛŋnde Havila len ni}} “The first river is called Pishon; it is the one (*ndi*) that flows around the land of Havilah” [Gen 2:11](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.11.BKG); {{kobilan la va belaŋɔmpum}} “the cities (*la*) were sixty” [Deut 3:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/DEU.3.4.BKG).

Note: {{a}} is both “you (sing.)” and “they”; {{la}} is both “you (plur.)” and the pronoun of the *lan* class; {{n}} is “we” and the pronoun of plurals in *-on*. The context removes the ambiguity.

## Pronouns and narrative markers

In narrative, the subject pronoun combines with the marker **mi** (“and then”):

| Form | Analysis | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {{mí}} + noun | “and (the noun)…” | {{Mí Hala dimi aa}} “And God said” [Gen 1:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.3.BKG) |
| {{mó}} | *mi* + *o* “and he/she” | {{mó yaa nda}} “and he brought them” [Gen 2:19](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.19.BKG) |
| {{má}} | *mi* + *a* “and they” | {{má kuumbuu}} “and they covered” [Gen 9:23](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.9.23.BKG) |
| {{mí n}}, {{mí la}}, {{mí ya i}} | *mi* + another pronoun | {{mí n komal Esau}} “and we met Esau” [Gen 32:7](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.32.7.BKG) |

In Southern Kissi, the same fusion gives *mi, ma, mbo, miŋ, mila, ma* [[(Childs1995, p. 75, 107)]]. The low-tone forms {{mò}}, {{mà}}, {{mì}} mark the **subjunctive** (“so that he…”); see [Tense, aspect, mood](../verbs/tense-aspect-mood.md) and [Tones](../../orthography/tone.md).

The independent pronoun also combines with the past vowel *oo* / *aa* (1979: *ndu oo*, *ya oo* [[(Keita1979, p. 87)]]). The Bible [[(BKG)]] writes it as a single word, with tones: {{ndóò}} “he, she (had)”, {{ndáà}} “they (had)”, {{yáà}} “I (had)”: {{yɔmndo ndu pilɛ bɛɛ ndóò vil-lɔ o lɛŋnden coo le}} “no tree had yet grown on the earth” [Gen 2:5](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.5.BKG).

## The independent pronoun as subject

The independent pronoun can replace the subject pronoun to **emphasize** the person [[(Keita1979, p. 86)]]: *Ndu yɔngu ya ni* “It is he who gave it to me”. In the Bible: {{Nda yɔŋnun kuubaa}} “It was they who became heroes” [Gen 6:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.6.4.BKG). The independent pronoun is often followed by the subject pronoun: {{Niaa pila bɛɛ la sina}} “you yourselves know” [Gen 31:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.31.6.BKG). Southern Kissi uses the same two constructions, for emphasis or contrast [[(Childs1995, p. 105-106)]].

!!! note "Comparison"
    In Southern Kissi, the subject pronouns are *í, à, ò, ŋ̀, là, à* and the object pronouns *yá, nùm, ndú, náá, ɲá, ndá* [[(Childs1995, p. 104-105)]]: the subjects have a low tone (except *í* “I”), the objects a high tone. The differences from the Bible lie mainly in the spelling (*ŋ̀* for {{n}} “we”) and in the 2nd person plural (*ɲá* for {{niaa}}). The Wikipedia article on Northern Kissi gives similar forms; it writes *y* for “I” before a vowel and *aa* for “they”, where the Bible writes {{i}} and {{a}}.
