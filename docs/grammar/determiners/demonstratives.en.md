# Demonstratives

Demonstratives point to a being or a thing. Kisiei has two series of them: one for what is **near** (“this”), the other for what is **far** or already mentioned (“that”). Like the other determiners, they **agree** with the class of the noun (see [Agreement](../agreement.md)).

## Form of the noun before the demonstrative

The texts of the Bible [[(BKG)]] confirm the rule described in 1979 [[(Keita1979, p. 57)]]:

- with the **near** demonstrative, the noun keeps its **full form**: {{kobio ho}}, {{vannda ha}}, {{pembei he}};
- with the **far** demonstrative, the noun takes its **stem form**: {{kobi kon}}, {{vanaa kan}}.

Southern Kissi has the same distribution: the near demonstrative follows the full form of the noun, the far one follows the stem (accompanied by the class pronoun outside the *o* class) [[(Childs1995, p. 110-111)]].

In the *i* class, the stem form before {{ken}} replaces *-ei* with **-ii**: {{pembei he}} / {{pembii ken}} “this/that mountain”, {{paangei he}} / {{paangii ken}} “this/that month” [Exod 12:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/EXO.12.3.BKG) [Gen 8:5](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.8.5.BKG).

In the *an*, *len* and *lan* classes, the noun is always in the stem form + class pronoun ({{mɛmma man}}, {{lɛŋnde len}}, {{kobila lan}}; see [Noun classes](../nouns/noun-classes.md)). Near and far are distinguished there by **tone** [[(Keita1979, p. 58)]], which the Bible does not mark. In Southern Kissi, the near demonstrative carries a falling tone and the far one a high tone [[(Childs1995, p. 110)]].

!!! rule "Spelling rule"
    - Near adjective: **{{ho}}**, **{{ha}}**, **{{he}}** (a single vowel): {{kobio ho}}.
    - Near pronoun, used on its own: **{{hoo}}**, **{{haa}}**, **{{hee}}** (double vowel): {{Hoo co}} “this one is…” [Luke 23:38](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/LUK.23.38.BKG).
    - The demonstrative is always written separately from the noun.

## Uses of the pronoun

The demonstrative used on its own replaces a noun:

- {{Hoo co masa Yuifia ni}} “This is the king of the Jews” [Luke 23:38](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/LUK.23.38.BKG);
- {{Le hee}} “because of this, that is why”, very frequent at the start of a sentence [Gen 2:21](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.21.BKG);
- {{O kon kɔɔli}} “after that” (*o* class), a narrative formula: {{O sɔɔn mun kɔɔli}} “after these events” [Gen 22:1](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.22.1.BKG).

!!! keita "1979 usage"
    In 1979, *ho* and *hoo* are given as a single form, and *tan*, *ten* as variants of *lan*, *len* after *l* [[(Keita1979, p. 57-58)]]. The Bible distinguishes *ho* (adjective) and *hoo* (pronoun) and does not use *tan*, *ten* as demonstratives. The description of Southern Kissi also gives a single form for the adjective and the pronoun (*hóò*) [[(Childs1995, p. 110)]].
