# Interrogatives

The rules on this page are drawn from the texts of the Kisiei Bible [[(BKG)]], compared with the 1979 description [[(Keita1979, p. 64-69)]].

## Question words

| Word | Meaning | Example (Bible) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {{nɛ̀ɛ́}} | who? (subject) | {{Nɛ̀ɛ́ cɔm yɛ nɔm}} “Who showed you…?” [Gen 3:11](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.3.11.BKG) |
| {{nɛ̀ɛ́-nɛ̀}} | who (is it)? | {{Nɔm nɛ̀ɛ́-nɛ̀}} “Who are you?” [Gen 27:18](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.27.18.BKG) |
| {{yɛɛ}} | what? which? | {{Dɛnɛ yɛɛ n tosa yɛ hoo}} “What have you done?” [Gen 3:13](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.3.13.BKG) |
| {{le yɛɛ}} | why? | {{Le yɛɛ n tambu yɛ kɔl}} “Why are you angry?” [Gen 4:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.4.6.BKG) |
| {{kuɛɛ}} | where?; which? | {{Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ}} “Where are you?” [Gen 3:9](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.3.9.BKG); {{Langbanɔ kuɛɛ co yɛ hoo}} “Who is this man?” [Gen 24:65](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.24.65.BKG) |
| {{vɛ̀ɛ̀}} | is it that…? how? | {{Vɛ̀ɛ̀ o co kɛndɛ lon}} “Is he well?” [Gen 29:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.29.6.BKG) |
| {{bàà}} | is it really…? | {{bàà mà tɛɛmbuu nda}} “will you really destroy them?” [Gen 18:24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.18.24.BKG) |

The same words exist in Southern Kissi: *kùɛ́ɛ́* “where”, *nɛ̀ɛ́* “who”, *yɛ̀ɛ́* “what”, *wɛ̀ɛ́* “how, how much” [[(Childs1995, p. 111, 264)]]; the initial *w* there corresponds to the *v* of the Bible ({{vɛ̀ɛ̀}}).

## Building the question

### Content questions (who, what, where…)

1. The question word is placed **at the start** of the sentence, or just after the noun it determines ({{Dɛnɛ yɛɛ}} “what thing”, {{Langbanɔ kuɛɛ}} “which man”).
2. It is often followed by the particle {{nɛ}}, which is optional and makes no notable difference in meaning (likewise in Southern Kissi [[(Childs1995, p. 144)]]): {{Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ}} “Where are you?”, {{Yɛɛ nɛ kendu yɛ a Mɛlɛka}} “Is anything too hard for the Lord?” [Gen 18:14](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.18.14.BKG). In Southern Kissi, this particle (*-nɛ́*) is optional and does not change the meaning [[(Childs1995, p. 111, 265)]].
3. The verb is followed by the particle **{{yɛ}}**, which closes the question: {{n co yɛ}}, {{n tosa yɛ}}. In the future, {{yɛ}} attaches to *-nin*: {{yɛnin}} ({{vɛ̀ɛ̀ ya sina yɛnin}} “how shall I know?” [Gen 15:8](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.15.8.BKG)).

The subject pronoun takes the form of the relative series: {{n}} “you (sing.)”, {{ya}} “I”, {{naa n}} “we”, {{niaa}} “you (plur.)” ({{Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ}} “Where are you?”; {{kuɛɛ niaa fula yɛ}} “where do you come from?” [Gen 29:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.29.4.BKG)). See [Relative clauses](../pronouns/relative.md).

The question-final particle is already described in 1979 (*yè*, “a particle that appears after the verb”) [[(Keita1979, p. 67)]]. Southern Kissi has the same particle *yɛ̀*, placed after the conjugated verb; it is absent when the clause has no conjugated verb [[(Childs1995, p. 111, 265)]].

### Yes/no questions

A yes/no question opens with {{vɛ̀ɛ̀}}: {{Vɛ̀ɛ̀ a yeema mì la kɔɛ niaa langbanɔɔ ho}} “Will you go with this man?” [Gen 24:58](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.24.58.BKG). Without a question word, only the rising voice marks the question; in writing, the question mark is enough [[(Keita1979, p. 69)]]. In Southern Kissi, this is the general case: a yes/no question differs from a statement only by its rising intonation [[(Childs1995, p. 264)]].

{{bàà}} introduces a question that expects a negative answer or expresses surprise [Gen 20:4](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.20.4.BKG).

!!! rule "Spelling rules"
    - Question words are written separately: {{le yɛɛ}}, {{Dɛnɛ yɛɛ}}.
    - {{nɛ̀ɛ́}} and {{vɛ̀ɛ̀}} carry their **tone marks**, as in the Bible; this distinguishes them from {{nɛɛ}} and {{vɛɛ}}.
    - The question ends with **?** with no space before it; inside quotation marks: {{«Kuɛɛ nɛ n co yɛ?»}}.

!!! keita "1979 usage"
    The 1979 description gives three interrogative adjectives (*köè* “which”, *ngö* “which” before the noun, *nèènè* “whose”) and three pronouns (*nèè* “who”, *kuè* “which one”, *ngö*) [[(Keita1979, p. 64-67)]]. The Bible uses {{nɛ̀ɛ́}} “who”, {{kuɛɛ}} “where, which” and {{yɛɛ}} “what”; the forms *köè* and *ngö* do not appear there and remain to be checked with speakers.
