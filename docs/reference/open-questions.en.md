# Open questions

These points cannot be settled from the written sources alone: the Bible [[(BKG)]] does not mark everything (in particular tones), the 1979 description [[(Keita1979)]] is sometimes incomplete or contradicted by the Bible, and Childs's description [[(Childs1995)]] concerns Southern Kissi. They are put to **speakers and teachers**; all observations are welcome (see the project repository).

## Spelling

1. **Tones.** Which tones should be marked, apart from the grammatical cases where the Bible marks them regularly ({{mí}} / {{mì}}, {{háá}}, {{ndóò}}, {{tùú}})? The lexical tones of common words are not described anywhere for the Kisiei of Guinea. See [Tone](../orthography/tone.md).
2. **Alphabetical order.** The order proposed in this guide for *ɛ*, *ɔ*, *ŋ*, *ɲ* and the digraphs needs to be validated. See [Alphabet](../orthography/alphabet.md).
3. **Words not in the Bible.** The respelled 1979 forms (status <span class="ks-badge ks-res">Respelled</span>) must be confirmed one by one, in particular vowel length and the final nasal (*n* or *ŋ*).

## Nouns and determiners

4. **Near and far in the *an*, *len*, *lan* classes.** According to the 1979 description, {{man}}, {{len}}, {{lan}} are used for “this…” and “that…”, distinguished by tone [[(Keita1979, p. 58)]]. To be checked.
5. **Irregular plurals.** The 1979 list of irregular plurals [[(Keita1979, p. 26-29)]] needs to be completed and checked.
6. **1st and 2nd person plural possessives** for the *i* and *-on* classes: forms not found in the Bible.
7. **1979 indefinites** *kèkè* “any” and *yööli* “all”, not found in the Bible: do they still exist, and how should they be written?
8. **1979 interrogatives** *köè* and *ngö* “which”, not found in the Bible.
9. **Negating a noun.** In Southern Kissi, the negated noun in “it is not X” takes its class pronoun [[(Childs1995, p. 261-262)]]. Is this the case in the Kisiei of Guinea?

## Verb

10. **Tone of the perfective and the habitual.** These two forms differ only in tone [[(Keita1979, p. 95)]]; a precise description of verbal tones is lacking.
11. **Stems of “go”.** The Bible uses {{kɔa}} in narrative and {{kɔɛ}} after {{mì}}; the 1979 description gives *köè* in the past and *köa* in the habitual. Which of the two distributions is that of everyday usage?
12. **The particle *-lɔ* / *-ndɔ* / *-yɔ*.** Its use for politeness is clear ({{yaŋ-ndɔ}}), but the Bible also uses it in negative sentences in the past ({{ndóò vil-lɔ … le}} [Gen 2:5](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.2.5.BKG)). What is this second use?
13. **Reciprocal.** The 1979 description gives long forms *-anndo*, *-lanndo* (*yilanndo*), not found in the Bible.
14. **Future with *co* and future with *-nin*.** What difference in meaning do speakers make between {{n co ndu dio}} and {{dianin}}?

## Varieties

15. **Regional variants.** The handwritten notes mention Kissidougou variants (*r* for *l*, other words). Which of them should be part of a common written usage? See [Regional varieties](../introduction/dialects.md).
