# Tales, proverbs and riddles

The annex of Sekou Sondo Keita's thesis [[(Keita1979Annexe)]] (“V- ANNEXE : Contes - Proverbes - Devinettes”, typed pages 166–184) brings together twelve tales in Kisiei. Each tale is followed by proverbs (*talelan*), each with its answer line (*kölöö*), and by riddles (*budon pialan*), whose answer follows the word *Mulio*.

For this guide the proverbs and riddles are **respelled in the Bible's orthography** (see [From the old to the new spelling](../orthography/old-and-new.md)); the form typed in 1979 is given in italics under each text. Words not found in the Bible are marked “respelled”, doubtful readings “to check” (see [How to read this guide](../introduction/how-to-read.md)); “[…]” marks a passage hidden by an ink stain.

The translations are the guide's own, unless marked “handwritten note”: the scanned copy carries glosses handwritten in French, some of them illegible ([?]); they are translated here from the French. Many of these texts are figurative and elliptic; the guide's translations are often approximate, and are marked (?) or “meaning uncertain” where they are.

Sources: [the scanned annex (PDF)](https://github.com/kebasaa/kisiei-language/blob/main/archive/Sekou%20Sondo%20Keita%20-%20kissie%20fondamental%20%28annexe%20proverbes%20contes%20devinettes%29.pdf) and [the archive's working notes](https://github.com/kebasaa/kisiei-language/blob/main/archive/Sekou%20Sondo%20Keita%20-%20kissie%20fondamental%20%28annexe%20proverbes%20contes%20devinettes%29.md), which give every proverb and riddle as typed and an outline of each tale.

## Proverbs (*talelan*)

### 1. The sons, the chieftaincy and the children (?)

{{Poaa}} {{lebooloa|?}} {{nda ŋiɔn}} (tale, p. 168)

**1.** {{Te yaw tembu le, vii pim te.}} — *kölöö*: {{Vana nɔ se le a tiindan caanda.}}
:   1979: *“Te yaw tembu le vii pim te”. — Kölöö : Vana nö se le a tiindan tyaanda.*
:   Meaning uncertain (word for word: “if *yaw* does not wear out, *vii* is not satisfied”). — Kölöö: “One who has no means puts his hope in others.” [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 168)]]

**2.** {{Cua doosunɔɔ nɔ ma}} {{hilu|r}} {{le, sɔlaa o co ni.}} — *kölöö*: {{Mɛɛ vana dɛli cuaambɔɔ yɛ, o nɔ kaa ni.}}
:   1979: *“Tyua dosunöö nö ma hilu le, sölaa o tyo ni”. — Kölöö : Mèè vana dèli tyuamböö yè o nö kaa ni.*
:   “The hunter's child does not crave meat: he always gets some.” — Kölöö: “As one trains a child, so he behaves.” [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 168)]]
:   Handwritten notes: “the hunter's child has no craving to eat meat because he [?]”; above *hilu*: “craving for meat”; under the answer: “as the child is used to, so he behaves”.

### 2. Sofɛlɛ and his master

{{Sofɛlɛ nda masanɔ ndɔɔ}} (tale, p. 169)

**3.** {{Vana velu pɛndua pɛ, a hini}} {{nika|r}} {{macali.}} — *kölöö*: {{Vana nɔ}} {{vɛŋaa|?}} {{ŋiɔn pɛ, fɔ ma biilu nda, nda ŋiɔn kpow}} {{nika|r}}.
:   1979: *“Vana velu pèndua pè, a hini nika matyali”. — Kölöö : Vana nö vènhaa nhiön pè, fö ma biilu nda, nda nhiön gbow nika.*
:   “He who has fathered twins lies thus, on his back (?).” — Kölöö: “One who has … children must honour them, them and all the children (?).” Meaning uncertain. [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 169)]]

**4.** {{Vana}} {{lem|?}} {{pɛ pangaa le}} {{muiyo|r}}, {{numpila|r}} {{kani: baw yinnden le}} {{bi|?}} {{pɛ,}} {{muiyo|r}} {{kɔɛnnun,}} {{numpila|r}} {{koma cal o bɛ.}} — *kölöö*: {{Vana kɛ}} {{mɔmɔɔ|r}} {{yaw le miallo pɛ, te miallo kɔɛnnun yɛ.}}
:   1979: *“Vana lem pè pangaa le muiyo, numpila kani : baw yinden le bi pè, muiyo köènnun, numpila koma tyal o bè.” — Kölöö : Vana kè mömöö yaw le miallo pè, te miallo köènnun yè.*
:   Meaning uncertain: it speaks of a field shelter, mosquitoes (*muiyo*) and another insect (*numpila*); when a fire is lit the mosquitoes flee, but the *numpila* stays (?). — Kölöö: “If one does not cook a meal for the stranger, the stranger leaves (?).” [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 169)]]

### 3. Vɛɛn and jealousy (?)

{{Vɛɛn|?}} {{paŋalaa}} (tale, pp. 170–171)

**5.** {{Nɛɛ vana kɛsi cuɛiyan nda, a vuu pow lon te.}} — *kölöö*: {{Nɛɛ vana kɛsi ɲɛ kɛndɔɔ va, a cɛl mi dɛnɛ vɔɔnda kɔɛ lon te.}}
:   1979: *“Nèè vana kèsi tyuèiyan nda, a vuu pow lon te”. — Kölöö : Nèè vana kèsi nyèkèndöö va, a tyèl mi dènèvöönda köè lon te.*
:   “Where oil has been set down, one does not throw stones.” — Kölöö: “Where good things have been put, one does not let bad things go.” [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 170)]]

**6.** {{Siaŋaa válán co naŋɔɔ le.}} — *kölöö*: {{Naŋɔɔ lahɔl co kaa kɛndɛ ko le.}}
:   1979: *“Sianhaa valan tyo nanhöö le”. — Kölöö : Nanhöö lahöl tyo Kaa kèndè ko le*
:   “Bright red is not (necessarily) beautiful.” — Kölöö: meaning uncertain (*Kaa* is underlined in the typescript). [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 170)]]

### 6. Leopard, Lion and Cuilin

{{Tamban kollo|r}}, {{yàlàá a}} {{Cuilin|r}} (tale, p. 174)

:   1979: *“ Visia mèè n va yè lende n yinga ni ”. — Kölöö : Vana löön takpa le nyè hiow mumndo le.*
:   “As you were (?), so you wake up (?).” — Kölöö: meaning uncertain (*takpa* “chest”, *ɲɛ hiow* “nothing surpasses”). [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 174)]]

**12.** {{Taala bɛndu co}} {{mɔmɔ|?}} {{bɛndu le.}} — *kölöö*: {{Yoomuma|?}} {{co le va o bɔɔ le.}}

## Riddles (*budon pialan*)

### 1. The sons, the chieftaincy and the children (?)

{{Poaa}} {{lebooloa|?}} {{nda ŋiɔn}} (tale, p. 168)

**1.** {{Piɔɔ sia o sia le, kpɛɛnɛi kon nɔ mɛnndan lɔ́ɔ́-lɔ́ɔ̀.}} — *mulio*: {{Lembulten|r}}
:   1979: *Piöö sia o sia le, kpèènèi kon nö mènndan löölöö. ( Mulio : Lombulten )*
:   “Rain or no rain, this pot always has water.” — The lemon. [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 168)]]
:   Handwritten notes: *Halaa* above *Piöö*; “the rain rains or does not, this pot always has water”; “dumbullen = lemon”. Answer typed *Lombulten*; the 1979 glossary has *Lembulten*.

**2.** {{Cɛi|?}} {{kala ni pɔmbɔ kandun yindɔɔ o lebol.}} — *mulio*: {{Hollo|r}}
:   1979: *Tyièi kala ni pömbö kandun yindöö o lebol. ( Mulio : Hollo )*
:   “Little mother's (the aunt's) house starts its roof at the neck.” — The mushroom. [[(Keita1979Annexe, p. 168)]]
:   Handwritten note: “the house of the little mother (aunt) starts with the head — mushroom”.
