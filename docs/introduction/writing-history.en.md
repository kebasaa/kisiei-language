# History of writing

## The spelling of the 1970s

After Guinea's independence, the national languages were taught from the Cultural Revolution of 1968 onwards; the Académie des Langues in Conakry published *Principes d'orthographe et de segmentation* (principles of spelling and word division) for Kisiei [[AcademiePrincipes]]. Keita's manual ([[Keita1979|1979]]) follows this spelling. It is based on the Latin alphabet of the French typewriter and renders the sounds specific to Kisiei with **letter groups** and **accented vowels**:

| Sound | 1970s spelling | Example (1979) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [c] (palatal “ch”) | *ty* | *tyuaa* “child” |
| [ŋ] | *nh* | *nhuèènu* “five” |
| [ɲ] | *ny* | *nyumndan* “night” |
| [ɛ] (open e) | *è* | *dènè* |
| [ɔ] (open o) | *ö* | *söö* “chicken” |

This spelling does **not mark tones**.

## The current spelling

The current spelling replaces these groups with single letters from the African reference alphabet: **c**, **ŋ**, **ɲ**, **ɛ**, **ɔ** [[NouvelleOrtho]]. The correspondence sheet kept in the archive also gives “*mb = nb*”; the Bible, however, always writes *mb* ({{pɔmbɔɔ}}, {{kumbu}}), and this is the usage the guide follows.

The **Kisiei Bible** [[(BKG)]] is the major text published in this spelling. It adds practices that the correspondence sheet does not mention:

- **ŋ at the end of a stem**, including before suffixes in *-nd-*: {{lɛŋnden}} “country” (1979: *lènnden*), {{naŋ}} “good” (1979: *nan*);
- **tones**, marked by accents on certain words only ({{Ɲɔ́ɔ́ téngá}} [Gen 1:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1394/GEN.1.3.BKG));
- **the hyphen**, which attaches relatives and class markers at the end of a clause ({{fula-o}}, {{nin-nda}}) and joins compound and reduplicated words ({{ɲɛ-ɲɛ}}, {{taw-taw}});
- **« » quotation marks** for reported speech.

The [Spelling](../orthography/index.md) chapter describes these rules, and the [correspondence table](../orthography/old-and-new.md) helps to rewrite a 1979 text in the current spelling.
