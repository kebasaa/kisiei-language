# The Kisiei language

**Kisiei** (also written *kissi*, *kisi* or *kisie*; ISO 639-3 code `kqs`) is the language of the Kisia (sing. {{Kisinɔɔ|r}}). It is also called **Northern Kissi** to distinguish it from Southern Kissi, spoken in Sierra Leone and Liberia. It belongs to the **Mel** branch of the Atlantic languages (Niger-Congo family); its closest relatives are Sherbro and Krim in Sierra Leone, as [[Homburger1941]] already noted.

## Where Kisiei is spoken

Kisiei is spoken [[(Keita1979, p. 6-8)]]:

- **in Guinea**, in the prefectures of **Guéckédou** (the mother tongue of about 97% of the inhabitants at the time), **Kissidougou** (more than two thirds) and in the south of **Faranah** prefecture (sub-prefectures of Bagna and Kobikoro);
- **in Sierra Leone**, in the districts of **Kailahun** (Bouliedou, Diah, Kama) and **Kono** (Soa, Toli, Gbané, Lei, Mafindo);
- **in Liberia**, in the **Wam** chiefdom, in the north of the country.

The Kissi region is an area of forest and farming; rice holds a central place there [[(Paulme1954)]]. The neighbouring languages (Maninka, Kuranko, Loma, Mende, Kono, Gbandi, Lele) have left loanwords, which differ from one variety to another (see [Regional varieties](dialects.md)).

## General features

What this guide describes in detail:

- **Noun classes** marked by suffixes: a noun ends in a vowel or in *-an*, *-lan*, *-len*, etc., and this suffix shows its class and number (see [Noun classes](../grammar/nouns/noun-classes.md)).
- **Agreement by repetition**: determiners, pronouns and relatives repeat the suffix of the noun; this is what is called the *alliterative structure* [[(Keita1979, p. 121-122)]] (see [Agreement](../grammar/agreement.md)).
- **Stem forms and full forms**: a noun followed by a determiner loses its suffix ({{cɛiyo}} “the house” → {{cɛi kon}} “that house”).
- **Verb stems that change their vowel** according to tense, mood and polarity (see [Stems and verb groups](../grammar/verbs/stems-and-groups.md)).
- **A tone language**, with two levels (high and low), which distinguish words and tenses; the spelling marks tones only in certain cases (see [Tone](../orthography/tone.md)).
- **Subject – verb – object word order**, with tense auxiliaries placed between the subject and the verb.
