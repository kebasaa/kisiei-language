# Regional varieties

In Guinea two main groups of varieties are distinguished [[(Keita1979, p. 8-10)]]. Speakers understand each other without difficulty: the grammar is the same, and the differences mainly concern the pronunciation of a few sounds and part of the vocabulary.

## Kpekeduei and Kisiduei

| Group | Region | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kpekeduei** (Guéckédou variety) | Guéckédou prefecture; south of Kissidougou (Kondiadou) | No *r* sound: people say *l*. Loanwords from Loma, Mende and Krio depending on the area. Three sub-varieties are distinguished: *Kisii malowwe* (west), *Luannde* (east) and *Sayaninnde* (centre). |
| **Kisiduei** (Kissidougou variety) | Kissidougou, Faranah | The *r* sound exists where Kpekeduei has *l*; loanwords from Maninka. |

Some correspondences (respelled forms) [[(Keita1979, p. 8-10)]]:

| Meaning | Kpekeduei | Kisiduei |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mat | {{valaa\|r}} | {{varaa\|r}} |
| bag | {{bɔlɔɔ}} | {{bɔrɔɔ\|r}} |
| wrapper (cloth) | {{kolaa}} | {{koraa\|r}}, {{tafeo\|r}} |
| elephant | {{kamaa\|r}} | {{sɛmbaa\|r}} |
| pig | {{pewvo}} | {{poovo\|r}} |

The handwritten annotations in the digitised copy [[Notes1979]] add other Kissidougou variants (marked “Kdg”): *doo* for {{dioo|r}} “eat”, *tyallo* for *kiallo* “doe”, *työndöö* for *kiöndöö* “pick”, *voröö* for *violöö* “give birth” (forms quoted in the old spelling). Similar alternations between *c* and *k* from one variety to another are reported elsewhere in the Kissi area [[(Childs1995, p. 30)]].

!!! rule "Rule"
    **The Bible follows the Guéckédou variety**: it writes *l* where Kissidougou pronounces *r* ({{kolaa}}, never *koraa*). The letter *r* appears in the Bible only in proper names and loanwords ({{Israɛl}}, {{Abraham}}). So write the form with *l*, even if you pronounce *r*.

The distribution of *r* is confirmed by the description of Southern Kissi: the North has a distinct *r*, whereas the South has only *l*, of which [r] is just a rare variant [[(Childs1995, p. 10, 33, 157)]].

## Words whose meaning changes from one variety to another

Some words that are common in one variety are avoided or have another meaning in the other [[(Keita1979, p. 8-10)]]; for example {{kundei|r}} “clod of earth” in Kissidougou, but an intimate meaning in Guéckédou. When in doubt, choose the term used in the Bible.

## Outside Guinea

The 1979 manual does not describe the varieties of Sierra Leone and Liberia. They are described, under the name *Southern Kissi*, in a grammar and a dictionary [[(Childs1995)]] [[(Childs2000)]], which this guide cites for comparison. A 1973 survey distinguishes three groups: *Duku* in Guinea, the northernmost and most divergent; *Kpekedu* (Guéckédou), close to the Toli and Leyi varieties of Sierra Leone; and the varieties of Liberia and Sierra Leone [[(Childs1995, p. 10, 318)]]. Guéckédou is assigned there to the Southern area. The same survey notes that stories in *Duku* were poorly understood by speakers of the Southern varieties [[(Childs1995, p. 10)]].

The differences noted between North and South concern vocabulary (“write”: *sɛwaa* in the North, *pooɲaa* in the South; the Bible writes {{sɛva}}), the *r* sound and a few phonological rules [[(Childs1995, p. 10, 318)]]. The Bible does not always follow the forms given for the North: it writes {{bollen}} “head”, like the South, and not *botteŋ* [[(Childs1995, p. 318)]].

This guide does not deal with these varieties in their own right. Speakers from these regions are invited to report the differences (see [Open questions](../reference/open-questions.md)).
